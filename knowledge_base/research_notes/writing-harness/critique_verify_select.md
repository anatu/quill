# Harness-level techniques for improving machine-written prose: critique-revise, verify-repair, generate-select, editing models

Reader notes on provenance. "Verified" means the number was read from the paper's full text during this research session (via an automated page reader, so table values should be spot-checked before being quoted in print). "From memory" means I am citing the paper from prior knowledge and did not re-open it; treat the figure as approximate. "Snippet only" means I saw a search summary and not the paper. Venue labels are given as [peer-reviewed: venue] or [preprint] or [blog].

## 1. Self-critique and iterative refinement (Self-Refine, Reflexion-style loops, debate, author-editor-critic, edit models) and when revision makes text worse

### Takeaway
Same-model critique-and-rewrite gives large gains on preference-judged, loosely specified generation tasks and roughly zero on tasks where the model must detect its own reasoning errors; the consistent finding across surveys is that feedback quality is the bottleneck, and that gains reported for intrinsic self-correction often vanish against a stronger single-pass prompt. LLM revision of prose reliably moves text toward a common "model voice" (fewer pronouns, more adjectives, large lexical replacement) even when told to make minimal or grammar-only edits, and repeated rewriting accumulates factual drift.

### Cited Findings

Self-Refine and its critiques
- Self-Refine (same model generates, critiques, rewrites, up to 4 iterations) on 7 tasks with GPT-3.5 / ChatGPT / GPT-4. Verified table: dialogue response 25.4 -> 74.6 (GPT-4, preference rate), sentiment reversal 3.8 -> 36.2, code readability 27.4 -> 56.2, acronym generation 30.4 -> 56.0, constrained generation (CommonGen-Hard coverage) 15.0 -> 45.0, code optimisation 27.3 -> 36.0, but math reasoning 92.9 -> 93.1 (GPT-4) and 64.1 -> 64.1 (GPT-3.5). None of the tasks is long-form expository prose. [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2023] — [Madaan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)
- Who judged in Self-Refine: preference tasks used GPT-4 as proxy judge (agreement with humans 82% sentiment, 68% acronym, 71% dialogue) plus a blind human A/B by the authors on 150 examples per dataset: sentiment 75.0% Self-Refine vs 21.4% direct; acronym 44.6% vs 12.2% (43.2% tie); dialogue 47.6% vs 19.7% (32.8% tie). — [Madaan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)
- Diminishing returns per iteration in Self-Refine (average over three models): code optimisation 22.0 -> 27.0 -> 27.9 -> 28.8; sentiment 33.9 -> 34.9 -> 36.1 -> 36.8; constrained generation 29.0 -> 40.3 -> 46.7 -> 49.7. Most of the gain is in the first revision. — [Madaan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)
- Feedback-quality ablation in Self-Refine: specific feedback 43.2 vs generic feedback 31.2 vs no feedback 0 on sentiment reversal (ChatGPT); 27.5 / 26.0 / 24.8 on code optimisation. On math, ChatGPT's self-feedback was "everything looks good" for 94% of instances; with oracle "is it wrong" feedback GPT-3.5 gained +4.8 points. — [Madaan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)
- Weak-writer / strong-critic result inside Self-Refine: Vicuna-13B could not run the loop itself (malformed feedback, repeated its own output), but with Vicuna-13B drafting math answers (24.18%) and ChatGPT supplying feedback and refinement accuracy rose to 40.5%. This is the closest early datapoint to "small model drafts, large model critiques". — [Madaan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)
- Negative replication. Intrinsic self-correction (no external signal) lowers accuracy: GPT-4 GSM8K 95.5 -> 91.5 -> 89.0 over two rounds; GPT-3.5 CommonSenseQA 75.8 -> 38.1; Llama-2 GSM8K 62.0 -> 43.5. With oracle labels deciding when to stop, the same loop helps (GPT-3.5 GSM8K 75.9 -> 84.3). [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2024] — [Huang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)
- Prompt confound in Self-Refine's constrained-generation result: Self-Refine's reported 44.0 -> 67.0 coverage (7 calls) is beaten by a single-pass prompt that states the constraint explicitly (81.8), and applying self-correction on top of that stronger prompt reduces coverage to 75.1. — [Huang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)
- Multi-agent debate vs equal-budget self-consistency (GSM8K, GPT-3.5): with 6 responses, debate 83.2 vs self-consistency 85.3; with 9 responses, 83.0 vs 88.2. The authors conclude debate's gains are an ensembling effect, not correction. — [Huang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)
- Critical survey (covers work to May 2024): no prior work shows reliable self-correction from self-generated feedback with in-context learning on general tasks; self-correction works when (a) reliable external feedback exists (tools, retrieval), (b) the model is fine-tuned at scale for it, or (c) the task is decomposable so that verification is easier than generation. Many positive results used weak initial prompts or oracle information. [peer-reviewed: TACL 2024] — [Kamoi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297)
- Earlier taxonomy survey of automated-feedback correction (training-time, generation-time, post-hoc), which is the standard census of this literature up to 2023. [peer-reviewed: TACL 2024] — [Pan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03188)
- Self-bias: across six LLMs (GPT-4, GPT-3.5, Gemini, LLaMA2, Mixtral, DeepSeek) on translation, constrained text generation and math, self-refinement improves fluency and understandability of outputs while amplifying the model's over-rating of its own output; larger models and accurate external feedback reduce the bias and are what produce real downstream gains. (Abstract-level; no effect sizes read.) [peer-reviewed: ACL 2024] — [Xu et al. 2024, "Pride and Prejudice"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11436)
- Related negative results on LLM self-critique in planning and graph colouring: GPT-4 critiquing its own solutions does not improve them and the content of the critique matters little; a sound external verifier is what helps. From memory. [preprints / workshop] — [Stechly et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12397); [Valmeekam et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08118)

Debate and author-editor-critic setups
- Multi-agent debate improved biography factuality in the original paper (single agent ~66% -> debate ~74% of bullet facts judged consistent; from memory, ChatGPT, 524 computer-scientist biographies). [peer-reviewed: ICML 2024] — [Du et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14325)
- A benchmark of debate protocols found multi-agent debate does not reliably outperform self-consistency or ensembling and is sensitive to hyperparameters. From memory. [peer-reviewed: ICML 2024] — [Smit et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17371)
- Mixture-of-Agents (several proposer models, an aggregator rewrites): AlpacaEval 2.0 65.1 vs GPT-4o 57.5 (from memory; LLM-judged). [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2025] — [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04692)
- Self-MoA: aggregating multiple samples from the single best model beats mixing different models, 65.7 vs 59.1 on AlpacaEval 2.0 (+6.6), and +3.8% average on MMLU / CRUX / MATH; mixing different LLMs "often lowers the average quality"; performance is more sensitive to proposer quality than to diversity. Verified. [preprint] — [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674)
- Multi-agent fiction pipelines: Agents' Room (planner and writer agents sharing a scratchpad) produced stories preferred by human raters over single-pass baselines on plot, creativity, development and language use, but the outputs show higher trigram repetition and lower unique-word share than human stories. Snippet only. [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2025] — [Huot et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02603)
- CritiCS (groups of LLM critics plus a leader refining plan and then text): human study reports higher creativity and engagement while coherence is maintained. Snippet only. [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2024] — [Bae and Kim 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02428)
- Re3 (plan, draft, rerank continuations for coherence and premise relevance, then an edit module for factual consistency) on 2,000+ word stories: human judges rated substantially more stories coherent (+14% absolute) and premise-relevant (+20% absolute) than a same-model rolling-window baseline (from memory, abstract figures). The reranking stage carried the gain; the detect-and-edit module contributed little in ablations (from memory). [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2022] — [Yang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06774)

Edit-based models and human-revision corpora
- IteraTeR: corpus of human iterative revisions (Wikipedia, ArXiv, news) annotated with edit intentions (fluency, clarity, coherence, style, meaning-changed); DELIteraTeR adds span detection so the model edits only where needed. From memory; I did not re-read the effect sizes. [peer-reviewed: ACL 2022 / In2Writing 2022] — [Du et al. 2022, IteraTeR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.03802); [Kim et al. 2022, DELIteraTeR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01816)
- PEER (plan-edit-explain-repeat, trained on Wikipedia edit histories) and EditEval (instruction-based editing benchmark). EditEval's headline observation (from memory) is that standard metrics for editing correlate poorly with each other and that models struggle most with edits requiring new information. [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2023 / preprint] — [Schick et al. 2022, PEER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.11663); [Dwivedi-Yu et al. 2022, EditEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.13331)
- CoEdIT: Flan-T5 fine-tuned on ~82K instruction-edit pairs (770M / 3B / 11B). Overall SARI 51.5 (xxl), 51.4 (xl), 49.8 (l) vs GPT3-Edit 41.8, InstructGPT 41.6, ChatGPT 36.9, PEER-11B 42.1. Human evaluation, 50 inputs, majority of 3 annotators: CoEdIT-xl preferred 64%, GPT3-Edit 10%, neither 22%. Sentence-level edits only (max length 256 tokens). Verified. [peer-reviewed: Findings of EMNLP 2023] — [Raheja et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09857)

Evidence that LLM revision homogenises style or strips voice
- Revision analysis on 86 pre-LLM student essays (ArgRewrite-v2), each with a human revision made after expert feedback, re-revised by gpt-5-mini, gemini-2.5-flash and claude-haiku under five instructions (general, minimal, grammar, completion, expansion). Human revisions: lexical Jensen-Shannon divergence about 0.2-0.3. gpt-5-mini: close to 3x that, many essays above 0.7; claude-haiku lowest, mean 0.476. Pronouns fall 40-60% in general revisions and 31-56% even under the "minimal edits" instruction; adjectives rise 57-90% (general) and 40-87% (minimal). Human edits scatter in embedding space; LLM edits shift every essay in a common direction, including under minimal and grammar-only prompts. Verified. [preprint, March 2026, Google DeepMind authors] — [How LLMs Distort Our Written Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18161)
- Same paper, user study (N=100 Prolific; 45 no-LLM, 55 with LLM): heavy LLM users rated their essays as less in their own voice (t(69)=5.80, p<0.001) and less creative (p=0.039); neutral-stance essays rose by 68.9% with extensive LLM use. Note the paper has internal inconsistencies between abstract and body on some figures. — [How LLMs Distort Our Written Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18161)
- Iterated rewriting accumulates distortion ("broken telephone"). Llama-3.1-8B and Mistral-7B, 100 iterations, 150 documents of 100-200 words per dataset. Pure English rephrasing chains (30 news documents): BLEURT against the original falls from 0.945 to 0.537 (Llama), 0.521 (Mistral), 0.490 (two models alternating), 0.427 (three models) at iteration 100; FActScore falls in the same ordering. Translation chains are worse (EN-FR BLEURT 0.949 -> 0.670; EN-TH -> 0.426). Lower temperature and more constrained prompts slow but do not stop the decay. Verified. [peer-reviewed: ACL 2025] — [Mohamed et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20258)
- Co-writing with an instruction-tuned LLM reduces content diversity across authors relative to writing alone or with a base model (argumentative essays; from memory). [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2024] — [Padmakumar and He 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05196)
- Access to generative-AI ideas made short stories individually better rated but more similar to each other (N~300 writers, 600 evaluators; from memory). [peer-reviewed: Science Advances 2024] — [Doshi and Hauser 2024](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290)
- Instruction-tuned LLMs differ systematically from human writers in grammatical and rhetorical features (for example, heavy use of present-participial clauses and nominalisations), and instruction-tuned models differ more than base models (from memory). [peer-reviewed: PNAS 2025] — [Reinhart et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.16107)
- Expert editing of LLM prose (LAMP corpus): 18 professional writers made 8,035 edits to 1,057 LLM paragraphs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Llama-3.1-70B); about 8 edits per roughly-200-word paragraph; 74% replacements, 18% deletions, 8% insertions; categories: awkward word choice 28%, poor sentence structure 20%, unnecessary exposition 18%, cliche 17%. No significant quality difference across the three models. Verified. [peer-reviewed: CHI 2025] — [Chakrabarty et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14509)
- Same paper, LLM-as-editor result: on 200 paragraph triplets ranked by 12 of the writers (3 per triplet, 600 rankings, Kendall's W 0.505), mean rank was writer-edited 1.5, LLM-edited 1.99, unedited LLM 2.5 (1 = best); LLM-edited beat unedited at p~1e-11. Giving the rewriter the experts' problem spans (oracle) did not help over LLM-detected spans, so the rewrite step, not detection, was limiting. Expert-expert span precision was only 0.57, and best LLM span detection 0.46. Hallucination and factual errors introduced by editing were not measured. — [Chakrabarty et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14509)
- Over-correction in grammatical error correction is a recognised LLM failure: LLMs rewrite for fluency beyond the minimal correction, lowering precision on minimal-edit benchmarks; recent work fine-tunes specifically for minimal-edit behaviour or samples several corrections and keeps only edits that recur across samples. Snippet only for both. [preprints] — [Adapting LLMs for Minimal-edit GEC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.13148); [edit-level voting](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.13624)
- Over-editing measured with known minimal patches in code: an explicit preservation instruction cut average excess edit distance from 0.195 to 0.131 and raised Pass@1 by 2.3 points on 400 BigCodeBench problems. Snippet only; code not prose. [preprint, Sept 2026] — [When Models Edit Too Much](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04061)

### Inferences
- For the requester's case the relevant regime is "external critic, decomposable check", which is the regime where the surveys say correction works; the regime shown to fail is the small model critiquing itself. The Vicuna-drafts / ChatGPT-critiques datapoint in Self-Refine points the same way, though on math rather than prose.
- Expect most of any revision gain in the first pass. Nothing I found supports more than one or two repair rounds for prose, and the broken-telephone and DeepMind results give a concrete reason to cap rounds: each strong-model rewrite moves text toward the strong model's register and loses some content fidelity.
- "Tell the editor to make minimal edits" is not sufficient by itself to preserve voice: pronoun and adjective shifts under a "minimal" instruction were nearly as large as under a general revision instruction. Span-restricted or diff-constrained editing (edit only flagged sentences, enforce an edit-distance budget, reject repairs that exceed it) is better supported than instruction alone, by analogy with the GEC and code over-editing results.
- The LAMP result is the best evidence that LLM editing of prose can be a net gain as judged by expert writers, but it tested same-register stylistic cleanup of generic LLM prose, not error repair on a styled draft, and did not check whether edits introduced errors.

### Gaps
- I found no study that measures critique-and-revise on textbook-style or other expository long-form prose with expert error annotation before and after. Published loops are evaluated on short generation tasks, fiction, QA or summaries.
- IteraTeR / DELIteraTeR / PEER / EditEval effect sizes were not re-read; cite the papers, not my numbers.
- No direct measurement of "loss of an individual author's voice" under LLM revision with authorship-verification metrics was located; the DeepMind preprint uses POS, lexical-divergence and embedding-shift proxies plus self-report.

## 2. Verification and factual repair (CoVe, RARR, FActScore / SAFE-guided revision, claim decomposition, minimal-edit correction)

### Takeaway
Decompose-verify-revise pipelines raise factual precision on long-form text by roughly 5 to 15 points absolute, usually at the cost of shorter, less informative output, and they do not address reasoning errors or steps that depend on a corrected claim. Minimal-edit repair preserves the original text far better than regeneration-style correction (about 90% intent preservation vs 6 to 40%) but fixes fewer errors per pass.

### Cited Findings
- Chain-of-Verification (draft, plan verification questions, answer them independently of the draft, write a revised answer) on long-form biographies with Llama 65B: FActScore 55.9 (few-shot baseline) -> 60.8 (joint) -> 63.7 (factored) -> 71.4 (factor+revise). Average facts per biography fell from 16.6 to 12.3: precision is bought partly by saying less. Chain-of-thought alone did not help (Llama-2 70B chat 41.3 zero-shot vs 41.1 CoT). Verified. [peer-reviewed: Findings of ACL 2024] — [Dhuliawala et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495)
- CoVe's stated limits: it addresses only directly stated factual inaccuracies, not flawed reasoning steps; it is capped by the base model's knowledge; keeping verification answers independent of the draft (factored) matters because a model that sees its draft repeats the draft's errors. — [Dhuliawala et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11495)
- RARR (question generation, retrieval, agreement check, minimal edit) vs two baselines, 150 test passages per dataset, human crowd raters. Attribution (human AIS) before -> after: NQ 35.4 -> 43.4, StrategyQA chains 24.5 -> 31.5, QReCC dialogue 13.2 -> 28.3. Intent preservation (human) 90.0 / 92.6 / 95.6%; Levenshtein preservation 89.6 / 89.9 / 80.2. Verified. [peer-reviewed: ACL 2023] — [Gao et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08726)
- The RARR comparison is the clearest edit-vs-rewrite trade-off in the literature: EFEC (a seq2seq fact corrector) reached higher attribution (NQ 48.3, SQA 51.7, QReCC 48.7) but preserved intent only 16.0 / 6.0 / 39.7% of the time because it condensed passages or dropped later sentences; LaMDA's research-and-revise preserved intent 26.0 / 40.0 / 21.3% and "changes writing style and intent". RARR changed roughly 10-20% of the text. — [Gao et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08726)
- RARR failure modes relevant to textbook prose: it does not revise downstream reasoning steps that depend on a corrected claim; it tends to keep unattributable claims rather than delete them; misleading evidence produces wrong edits; on StrategyQA all revision systems lowered downstream answer accuracy (by up to 2.6%). — [Gao et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08726)
- FActScore (atomic-fact precision against a knowledge source): human-annotated ChatGPT 58.3%, InstructGPT 42.5%, PerplexityAI 71.5% on biographies; ChatGPT falls from about 80% on frequent entities to about 16% on rare ones; later facts in a generation are significantly less precise; human annotators agreed on 88-96% of facts (91% overall); human annotation cost about $4 per generation; the automatic estimator is within 2 points of human. Verified. [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2023] — [Min et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)
- SAFE (LLM splits a response into facts, issues search queries, rates each): agrees with crowd annotators on 72.0% of 16,011 facts; on 100 sampled disagreements SAFE was right 76% and humans 19%; $0.19 per response vs $4 for humans. Introduces F1@K to penalise the "say less" route to precision. Verified. [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2024] — [Wei et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)
- Self-reflection as a repair method can cut informativeness: in the Integrative Decoding comparison a self-reflection baseline lowered Recall@128 on LongFact by 25.9% for one model. Verified. [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2025] — [Cheng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01556)
- FactEdit (post-editor trained on synthetic infilled errors) reports factuality gains of about 11 points on CNN/DM and about 31 on XSum (entailment-based metric) for abstractive summaries. Snippet only. [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2022] — [Balachandran et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.12378)
- Prompted LLM post-editing of summaries "often refused to edit or changed content significantly when it did"; mask-then-correct editors suffer over-erasure when detection is imprecise. Snippet only. [preprint; Findings of EMNLP 2023] — [arXiv 2501.11273](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2501.11273); [PivotFEC](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/2023.findings-emnlp.667.pdf)
- A 2026 minimal-edit correction preprint reports normalised edit distance 0.14 for a RARR baseline vs under 0.01 for its method, and 0.00 on already-correct summaries. Snippet only, single preprint. [preprint] — [MECV, arXiv 2608.05302](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/2608.05302v1)
- Small specialised checkers can stand in for a large model as the entailment judge: MiniCheck reports GPT-4-level accuracy on grounding-document fact checks at about 400x lower cost (from memory). [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2024] — [Tang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774)
- Sampling-based consistency as a hallucination detector without any reference: sentences not supported by other samples from the same model are likely hallucinated (SelfCheckGPT; from memory). [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2023] — [Manakul et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08896)
- Human detection of unfaithful claims in long outputs is itself costly and LLM checkers are weak at it: on 3,158 annotated claims from 130 book summaries, the best LLM rater (Claude 3 Opus with the entire book as evidence) reached 58.2 F1 on unfaithful claims vs 95.5 on faithful ones; unfaithful claims mostly required indirect reasoning (50.2%). Omission and chronology errors, which claim-level checks miss, were flagged in 52-85% and 33-62% of summaries respectively. Verified. [peer-reviewed: COLM 2024] — [Kim et al. 2024, FABLES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01261)

### Inferences
- Claim-level verification is well matched to "detail attached to the wrong claim" only if the plan or source states the correct pairing; these pipelines check whether each atomic claim is supported, and a mis-attached detail produces an unsupported atomic claim, so it should be detectable. They are poorly matched to "missing step" and "stops early", which are recall failures invisible to precision metrics (FABLES, SAFE's F1@K motivation).
- The evidence for minimal-edit repair preserving surface form is strong (RARR), but "style" in these papers means intent and edit distance, not register or authorial voice. No verification paper I found measures register preservation after repair.
- RARR's observation that corrections are not propagated to dependent sentences is a specific risk for explanatory prose where later sentences build on earlier ones; a repaired passage needs a second coherence check.
- Absolute error counts stay high after repair: the best long-form result (CoVe) still leaves about 29% of atomic facts unsupported, from a 44% starting point. A draft with two major errors per 300 words should not be expected to reach zero through one verify-repair pass.

### Gaps
- No study found reports major-error counts per passage for expository or textbook prose before and after verify-and-repair; the benchmarks are biographies, QA rationales, dialogue and summaries.
- Logical errors (missing steps, invalid inference) are explicitly out of scope for CoVe, RARR, FActScore and SAFE. I found no long-form "logical-gap repair" method with measured gains.
- FactEdit, ZeroFEC and PivotFEC numbers were not read from the papers.

## 3. Plan-conformance and coverage checking

### Takeaway
Planning before writing is well studied and helps coverage and organisation; explicit check-against-plan-and-regenerate loops with measured gains are scarce, and I found no standard plan-adherence metric. The nearest tools are QA-based and NLI-based faithfulness metrics repurposed with the plan as the source.

### Cited Findings
- Blueprint planning: generating an ordered list of question-answer pairs as a plan and conditioning the text on it makes output more faithful to the plan and source and allows QA-based checking of each plan item against the text (from memory). [peer-reviewed: TACL 2023] — [Narayan et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00397)
- QuestEval: reference-free QA-based metric that checks both precision (questions from the output answered by the source) and recall (questions from the source answered by the output); the recall direction is the published mechanism closest to "is every plan item covered" (from memory). [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2021] — [Scialom et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12693)
- STORM (multi-perspective question asking, outline, then section writing with retrieval): outlines and articles judged by experienced Wikipedia editors as better organised (+25% absolute) and broader in coverage (+10% absolute) than an outline-driven RAG baseline; editors also flagged transfer of source bias and over-association of unrelated facts (from memory, abstract figures). [peer-reviewed: NAACL 2024] — [Shao et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14207)
- Plan-guided retrieval before writing gives more informative responses with a higher rate of attribution to sources; factuality measured with sentence-level entailment as a simplified FActScore. Snippet only. [peer-reviewed: EMNLP 2024] — [Godbole et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10490)
- Plan-and-Refine: generate several candidate plans, write a response per plan, select one, then iteratively refine it for comprehensiveness and factuality within the plan. Snippet only; no numbers read. [peer-reviewed: ICTIR 2026 per search listing] — [UMass CIIR publication](https://ciir-publications.cs.umass.edu/pub/web/getpdf.php?id=1533)
- Re3's rerankers score candidate continuations for relevance to the plan/premise and coherence with prior text and keep the best; this plan-relevance reranking, not the edit step, produced its human-judged gains (see Section 1). — [Yang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06774)
- Constraint-coverage checking with regenerate-on-failure is the one setting where a plain "state the constraints clearly" single-pass prompt was shown to beat a check-and-revise loop (81.8 vs 75.1 concept coverage on CommonGen-Hard). — [Huang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)
- Content-selection failures are common and under-detected: omissions flagged in 52-85% of long summaries and chronology errors in 33-62%, found through free-text annotator comments rather than claim-level labels. — [Kim et al. 2024, FABLES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01261)

### Inferences
- A per-plan-item entailment or QA check (does the passage entail item k; is each item's detail attached to the right item) is a straightforward composition of published parts (blueprint QA pairs, QuestEval-style recall, NLI/MiniCheck entailment) but, as far as I could find, has not been evaluated as a regenerate-on-failure loop on expository text.
- Truncation ("stops early") is cheaply caught by plan-item recall; this is the failure class where a plan check should be most reliable, and where claim-precision checks contribute nothing.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed plan-adherence metric with reported human agreement was located.
- No measurement of how often regenerate-on-plan-failure converges, or of the pass rate as a function of retries, for long-form prose.

## 4. Candidate generation and selection (rerank, MBR, judge-guided best-of-N, small-generates / large-selects, cascades)

### Takeaway
Sampling many candidates raises the chance that a good one exists roughly log-linearly in N, but realised gains are limited by the selector: without a sound verifier, selection plateaus early (around 20 to 100 samples in the studies found, and most of the gain arrives by N of about 4 to 16). Consensus-style selection (MBR with a judge as utility) beats picking the judge's top-scored sample, and errors that are shared across samples or across model families are not removed by either.

### Cited Findings
- Coverage vs selection ("Large Language Monkeys"): the fraction of problems solved by any sample grows with N following an exponentiated power law (for example SWE-bench Lite with DeepSeek-Coder-V2 15.9% at 1 sample -> 56% at 250). But where no automatic verifier exists, majority voting and reward-model best-of-N plateau around 100 samples: on MATH with Llama-3-8B, selection accuracy moved only 40.50% -> 41.41% from 100 to 10,000 samples while coverage kept rising to 98.44%. Verified. [preprint] — [Brown et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)
- Same paper, cheap-model-many-samples vs strong-model-one-sample: five attempts with an open model solved 29.62% of SWE-bench Lite issues for $10.8 vs one attempt of GPT-4o 24.00% at $39 and Claude 3.5 Sonnet 26.70% at $51. This depends on having unit tests as the verifier. — [Brown et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)
- MBR with an LLM judge as the utility (each candidate scored against the other candidates as pseudo-references, N=30 at temperature 0.3): +3.6% AlpacaEval 2.0 and +0.28 MT-Bench on average over five generators with a 7B judge (Prometheus-2); gains persist for 70B generators with the small judge; performance rises with N and plateaus around N=20. Verified. [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2025] — [Wu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02902)
- MBR vs judge best-of-N in the same paper (MT-Bench average delta vs greedy): Prometheus-2-7B BoN +0.13 vs MBR +0.28; Llama-3-70B judge BoN +0.06 vs MBR +0.41; JudgeLM-7b BoN -0.01 vs MBR +0.22. Picking the judge's top-scored candidate was the weaker strategy for every judge tested. Lexical utilities help less: ROUGE-1 MBR +1.33 and BERTScore MBR +1.50 on AlpacaEval vs +3.62 for the judge utility; an embedding-similarity utility was negative (-1.42). The evaluation itself is LLM-judged. — [Wu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02902)
- Earlier MBR results for LLM text generation, and the observation that self-consistency, output ensembling and reranking are all special cases of MBR (from memory). [preprints / workshop] — [Suzgun et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.07634); [Bertsch et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01387)
- Universal Self-Consistency (give all N samples to the LLM and ask for the most consistent) extends self-consistency to free-form text (from memory) [preprint] — [Chen et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17311); but in a later comparison it did not improve reliably with more samples and sometimes got worse (LLaMA2 -4.4 on TruthfulQA), and on LLaMA3 biographies reached 27.9% accuracy vs 42.0% for Integrative Decoding. Verified. — [Cheng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01556)
- Integrative Decoding (sample k responses, then decode once while conditioning on each sample and summing log-probabilities, so content shared across samples wins): across six models, +1.1 to +15.4 points on biography accuracy and +1.6 to +8.5 on LongFact F1@128; gains are roughly log-linear in k with visible gains at k=4 (tested 1, 4, 8, 12, 16). Requires logit access to the generator. Verified. [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2025] — [Cheng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01556)
- Small specialist drafts, larger generalist verifies (Speculative RAG): a fine-tuned Mistral-7B drafter produces 5-10 answer drafts with rationales from different document subsets; Mixtral-8x7B scores drafts without fine-tuning. Accuracy vs the large model doing standard RAG: +12.97 points PubHealth, +3.86 PopQA, +2.15 MuSiQue, +2.14 ARC-Challenge, +0.33 TriviaQA, with latency down 12-51%. More drafts (1 to 20) improved accuracy. Short-answer QA, not prose. Verified. [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2025] — [Wang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08223)
- More calls are not monotonically better: voting-style compound systems can get worse as calls increase, because performance rises on "easy" queries and falls on "hard" ones (from memory). [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2024] — [Chen et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02419)
- Sample diversity vs quality: aggregating repeated samples of the best single model beats mixing models (Self-MoA, +6.6 on AlpacaEval 2.0), i.e. candidate quality dominates candidate diversity. — [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674)
- Correlated failures across models: over 349 models (HuggingFace leaderboard) and 71 models (HELM), when two models are both wrong they pick the same wrong answer 42.3% and 60% of the time vs 12.7% and 33% expected by chance; correlation is higher for same-provider, same-architecture and more accurate model pairs; LLM judges inflate the measured accuracy of models whose errors they share. Multiple-choice data, not prose. Verified. [peer-reviewed: ICML 2025] — [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)
- A judge's global correlation with reference scores can overstate its ability to pick the best of N candidates for one prompt, because within-prompt discrimination is a different quantity from across-prompt correlation. Secondary summary only; I did not locate the paper itself. [preprint, via review site] — ["When LLM Judge Scores Look Good but Best-of-N Decisions Fail" (summary)](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/when-llm-judge-scores-look-good-but-best-of-n-decisions-fail)
- NLI-based reranking of candidate summaries for correctness was an early negative result: off-the-shelf NLI models did not reliably rank the correct candidate first (from memory). [peer-reviewed: ACL 2019] — [Falke et al. 2019](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1213/)
- Cascades and routing address cost, not writing quality: FrugalGPT reports matching GPT-4 with up to 98% cost reduction on classification-style QA, RouteLLM over 2x cost reduction at comparable quality, AutoMix self-verification-triggered escalation (all from memory). [peer-reviewed: TMLR 2024 / ICLR 2025 / NeurIPS 2024] — [Chen et al. 2023, FrugalGPT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.05176); [Ong et al. 2024, RouteLLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.18665); [Aggarwal et al. 2023, AutoMix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12963)

### Inferences
- If the small model's errors were independent across samples at an average of two major errors per passage, a Poisson model gives roughly a 13.5% chance that a sample is error-free, so the chance that at least one of N is clean is about 44% at N=4, 69% at N=8 and 90% at N=16. This is my arithmetic, not a literature result, and it is an upper bound: the correlated-error and plateau findings say errors are shared across samples (a systematic mis-binding will recur), and realised yield is capped by the checker's ability to recognise the clean sample.
- The literature supports using the checker for consensus or per-item verification rather than a single holistic score: MBR beat top-score selection for every judge, and holistic best-of-N was the weakest selector.
- Selecting among whole passages discards good parts of rejected candidates. Sentence- or item-level recombination (Integrative Decoding, edit-level voting, Speculative RAG's per-draft rationale scoring) is the direction with measured long-form factuality gains, but recombination by a different model reintroduces the voice problem from Section 1; recombination done by the small model's own decoder (Integrative Decoding needs only its logits) would keep the register.
- Evidence on scaling suggests N in the range 4 to 16 captures most of the benefit; no study found supports N above about 20-30 for free-text selection without a sound verifier.

### Gaps
- No study found measures best-of-N for long-form prose with human-annotated error rate as the outcome and N as the independent variable.
- No direct measurement of within-model error correlation across temperature samples for long-form text (the correlated-errors paper is across models on multiple choice).
- "Small fine-tuned style model generates, large model selects" for prose quality specifically: nothing found. Speculative RAG is the closest architecture and is short-answer QA.

## 5. Division of labour: large-model-as-editor of small-model drafts, small style model rewriting large-model drafts, style transfer and "humanizer" post-edits

### Takeaway
Direct evidence on splitting content correctness and voice between two models is thin. What exists says: fine-tuning produces voice that prompting does not (expert readers strongly prefer fine-tuned output and detectors do not flag it), strong-model rewriting pulls text back toward the generic register, and paraphrase or humanizer passes preserve gist while degrading quality and precision, with no study measuring their effect on factual error rate in expository prose.

### Cited Findings
- Fine-tuning vs prompting for voice. Preregistered study, 28 MFA-trained writers and 516 general readers, 10,920 pairwise judgments, excerpts up to 450 words emulating 50 authors. In-context prompting (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro): experts preferred human text strongly (odds ratio 0.16 for stylistic fidelity, 0.13 for writing quality). Author-specific fine-tuned GPT-4o: experts preferred the AI text (OR 8.16 fidelity, 1.87 quality); general readers OR 16.65 and 5.42. Pangram flagged 97% of in-context outputs as AI and 3% of fine-tuned outputs. Verified. [preprint, v4 March 2026] — [Chakrabarty et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13939)
- Base (non-instruct) model text is not flagged by detectors: Llama3-8B base continuations of human prefixes scored 96.7% (GPTZero) and 98.8% (Pangram) "human" vs 30.3% and 17.1% for the instruct model. The same paper's humanizer (LoRA-tuned base model applied as an iterative paraphraser for 10 rounds, "HIP") reports a trade-off curve between semantic preservation (GPT-5-nano judge, 0-10) and detector human-likeness, with only DIPPER among baselines retaining a non-trivial trade-off. Aggregate numbers are in figures only. Verified text; [preprint, May 2026] — [Xu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19516)
- Audit of 19 humanizer and paraphrasing tools: typical humanizers lowered overall quality while keeping intent; low-quality ones often distorted meaning. Qualitative assessment by the authors (who work at a detection company). Snippet only. [peer-reviewed workshop: GenAIDetect 2025] — [Masrour et al. 2025, DAMAGE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03437)
- Paraphrasing as detector evasion: an 11B paraphraser (DIPPER) dropped DetectGPT's detection rate from 70.3% to 4.6% at 1% false-positive rate "without appreciably modifying the input semantics" as measured by automatic similarity (from memory). [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2023] — [Krishna et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.13408)
- Expert human detectors are robust to this: a majority vote of five annotators who use LLMs heavily misclassified 1 of 300 articles, including paraphrased and humanized ones (from memory). [peer-reviewed: ACL 2025] — [Russell et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15654)
- Small style-transfer model as post-editor: TinyStyler (about 800M parameters, authorship embeddings, reconstruct-from-paraphrase training) reports beating GPT-4 on authorship style transfer; the un-distilled version had strong style scores but weak meaning preservation, and self-distillation plus reranking of candidates was needed to bring meaning preservation up to LLM-level. Automatic metrics; sentence- to short-passage-level. Snippet only. [peer-reviewed: Findings of EMNLP 2024] — [Horvitz et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15586)
- Small trained corrector on top of a large model: LMCor trains a compact model to merge and correct several candidate outputs from an LLM and reports gains over the LLM's own outputs on GEC, data-to-text, summarisation and MT (from memory; not re-read). [peer-reviewed: EACL 2024] — [Vernikos et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.13514)
- Strong-model edits of generic LLM prose improve expert preference but stay well short of human editing (mean rank 1.99 vs 1.5 for writer-edited, 2.5 unedited), with the same model family generating and editing. — [Chakrabarty et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14509)
- Strong-model revision of human prose shifts it toward a shared model style even under minimal-edit instructions (see Section 1); among the three small production models tested, claude-haiku changed the text least (mean lexical JSD 0.476 vs human-revision baseline 0.2-0.3). — [How LLMs Distort Our Written Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18161)
- Each additional rewriting hop, and each additional model in the chain, increases content drift (BLEURT at 100 rephrasings: one model 0.537, two 0.490, three 0.427). — [Mohamed et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20258)
- Strong critic repairing a weak drafter's output can lift accuracy substantially when the weak model cannot self-correct (Vicuna-13B 24.18% -> 40.5% with ChatGPT feedback and refinement, math). — [Madaan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)

### Inferences
- The two orderings are not symmetric in the evidence. "Styled draft, strong model repairs" risks register drift in proportion to how much the strong model rewrites (DeepMind, LAMP); "accurate draft, style model rewrites" risks reintroducing errors, since style-transfer and paraphrase models show a consistent style-strength vs meaning-preservation trade-off (TinyStyler pre-distillation, HIP, DAMAGE), and the requester's style model already mis-binds details when generating freely. Neither ordering has a published long-form error-rate measurement.
- A workable reading of the evidence is that the strong model's role should be confined to detection, localisation and the smallest possible replacement spans (or to rejecting and resampling), since every study of strong-model rewriting shows register convergence, and the fine-tune-vs-prompt study shows that the register is the thing prompting cannot supply.
- Reranking is used inside the style-transfer literature itself to recover meaning preservation (TinyStyler), which is the same generate-several-then-filter pattern the requester proposes, applied in the reverse direction.

### Gaps
- No paper found evaluates "model A supplies correctness, model B supplies voice" as an explicit pipeline on long-form expository text with both error counts and expert style judgments.
- No study found measures factual or logical error rates introduced by humanizer or style-transfer passes; evaluations use semantic-similarity scores or holistic quality.
- STYLL and other LLM-prompted authorship-transfer results were not retrieved.

## 6. Constrained and guided decoding for writing

### Takeaway
Decoding-time interventions are effective at removing surface patterns (phrase-level "slop") and can raise factual precision modestly when they exploit agreement across samples or contrast between layers or models; none is shown to fix content-binding or missing-step errors, and blunt constraints (large token bans) damage quality.

### Cited Findings
- Antislop sampler: on detecting a banned phrase it backtracks to the phrase's first token, down-weights it and resamples; suppresses lists of 8,000+ patterns with writing-quality score at or above baseline, whereas plain token banning collapses writing quality to 28/100 at 8k patterns. Cost: throughput falls 69% with a 1,000-pattern list and up to 96% at 8,000. Verified. [preprint, Oct 2025] — [Paech et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)
- Same paper, training alternative (FTPO, logit-level fine-tuning on gemma-3-12b): 83-92% suppression with writing-quality score 67.3-68.9 vs 67.8 baseline, MMLU within about 3 points, GSM8K within about 1; DPO achieved 81-82% suppression but dropped writing quality to 54.6-63.0 and long-form writing from 51.3 to 26.9 at the 8k list. Quality scores are LLM-judged rubric scores. Some slop patterns occur over 1,000x more often in LLM than in human text. — [Paech et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061)
- Integrative Decoding (self-consistency implemented in the decoder): +1.1 to +15.4 points biography accuracy and +1.6 to +8.5 LongFact F1@128 over six models, improving with k. — [Cheng et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01556)
- Contrastive decoding (maximise the gap between a large "expert" and small "amateur" model's log-probabilities) improves coherence and reduces repetition and topic drift in open-ended long-form generation by automatic and human evaluation (from memory). [peer-reviewed: ACL 2023] — [Li et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15097)
- DoLa (contrast final-layer and earlier-layer logits) reports factuality gains on TruthfulQA and FACTOR without retrieval (from memory; short-form benchmarks). [peer-reviewed: ICLR 2024] — [Chuang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03883)
- Factual-nucleus sampling: because sampling randomness hurts factuality more in the latter part of a sentence, decay the nucleus p within each sentence; reported to improve the factuality-vs-diversity trade-off for open-ended generation (from memory). [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2022] — [Lee et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.04624)
- Lexically constrained decoding (NeuroLogic) guarantees inclusion or exclusion of specified words with fluency comparable to unconstrained beam search on constrained-generation tasks (from memory; pre-LLM, sentence-level). [peer-reviewed: NAACL 2021] — [Lu et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12884)
- Temperature interacts with drift under repeated rewriting: near-zero temperature stabilises factuality after the first two iterations, temperature 1.0 gives the steepest decline. — [Mohamed et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20258)
- Later positions in a long generation are less factual (FActScore position analysis), which is the empirical basis for position-dependent decoding and for checking late content harder. — [Min et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251)

### Inferences
- For a locally hosted small model, the decoder-side options with long-form evidence are Integrative Decoding (needs only the model's own logits and several samples) and sentence-position-aware sampling; both act on the small model itself and therefore do not change register, unlike strong-model repair.
- Antislop-style backtracking is relevant to the strong model's generic register only if the strong model's logits are accessible, which is not the case for an API model; for the small model it is a style control, not an error control.

### Gaps
- I found no NLI- or entailment-guided decoding method with long-form prose results after 2023; recent work has moved to post-hoc verification.
- No paper found reports whether any decoding intervention changes the rate of mis-attributed details or missing steps specifically.
- Antislop's quality claims rest on an LLM-judged rubric; no human evaluation was reported in the text I read.

## 7. LLM judges as the checker: validity limits and human-agreement figures

### Takeaway
LLM judges agree with humans about as well as humans agree with each other on holistic preference, but they favour text that is familiar to them (their own or low-perplexity), are order-sensitive, share errors with related models, and are much weaker at finding unfaithful claims than at confirming faithful ones. For the requester this matters in a specific direction: a Claude checker comparing a Claude-register passage with a small-model passage is biased toward the former, and its error detection in long text has documented recall limits.

### Cited Findings
- Self-preference and self-recognition: GPT-4, GPT-3.5 and Llama-2 all rate their own summaries above other models' and humans'; GPT-4 distinguishes its own summaries at 73.5% accuracy out of the box; fine-tuning for self-recognition (to above 90%) increases self-preference linearly (Kendall's tau 0.41 -> 0.74 for GPT-3.5 on XSUM). Order of presentation flipped pairwise preferences 25% of the time for GPT-4, 58% for GPT-3.5 and 89% for Llama-2. 1,000 articles per dataset. Verified. [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2024] — [Panickssery et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)
- Mechanism: across eight LLMs, judges assign higher scores to lower-perplexity text than human evaluators do, whether or not the text was self-generated; self-preference is explained as preference for familiar text. Snippet only. [workshop: NeurIPS 2024 Safe GenAI] — [Wataoka et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819)
- Self-refinement loops amplify self-bias and improve fluency more than quality across six LLMs; external feedback reduces it. [peer-reviewed: ACL 2024] — [Xu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.11436)
- Shared errors inflate judged accuracy: judges mark as correct the wrong answers they would also give, particularly for same-provider or same-architecture models; the authors recommend calibrating each model-judge pair against ground truth. [peer-reviewed: ICML 2025] — [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)
- Holistic agreement is high: GPT-4 pairwise judgments agreed with human experts 85% of the time vs 81% human-human on MT-Bench, with documented position, verbosity and self-enhancement biases (from memory). [peer-reviewed: NeurIPS 2023 D&B] — [Zheng et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)
- Position bias can decide outcomes: reordering responses made one system win 66 of 80 comparisons under a ChatGPT judge (from memory). [preprint / ACL 2024] — [Wang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17926)
- Error detection is much harder than preference: best LLM rater 58.2 F1 on unfaithful claims vs 95.5 on faithful claims in book-length summarisation (723 claims, 69 unfaithful), with the full book in context. [peer-reviewed: COLM 2024] — [Kim et al. 2024, FABLES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01261)
- Human agreement on atomic-fact support labels is 88-96% when a knowledge source is given (FActScore), but crowd labels were wrong in 76 of 100 sampled disagreements with a search-augmented LLM rater (SAFE), so "human agreement" depends heavily on annotator effort and access to evidence. — [Min et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14251); [Wei et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18802)
- Human agreement on locating problems in prose is low: expert writers' span-level precision against each other was 0.57 (any problem) and 0.23 (same category); best LLM 0.46 and 0.21; ranking agreement Kendall's W 0.505. — [Chakrabarty et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14509)
- GPT-4 as a proxy judge of generation quality agreed with humans 68-82% depending on task in Self-Refine's own validation. — [Madaan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)
- Surveys of LLM-as-judge methods and biases (taxonomy of position, verbosity, self-enhancement, fluency and familiarity effects; calibration and multi-judge remedies). [preprints, widely cited] — [Gu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594); [Li et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05579)

### Inferences
- A Claude judge is on firmer ground when asked narrow, evidence-anchored questions (does the passage entail plan item k; which sentence carries detail d) than when asked which passage is better: the first resembles FActScore/SAFE-style claim checking where agreement with careful humans is high; the second is where familiarity bias operates, and the generic-register candidate will be the low-perplexity one for the judge.
- Because error-detection recall is the weak side (58 F1 on unfaithful claims at best in FABLES), a passage passing the check is weaker evidence than a passage failing it. The checker's miss rate on the requester's own error taxonomy has to be measured against human labels; the literature gives no transferable figure.
- Randomising candidate order and hiding provenance are low-cost controls with direct support (order flipped up to 25% of GPT-4 preferences; source labels changed GPT-4's self-preference from 0.32 to 0.73).

### Gaps
- No study found reports Claude-family self-preference magnitudes specifically, or judge bias between a base-model-register text and an assistant-register text.
- No inter-annotator agreement figure was found for "major logical or factual errors per passage" in textbook-style prose; the closest are atomic-fact support (88-96%) and prose-problem span precision (0.57).

## 8. Census and trend

### Takeaway
Verification / factual-repair and LLM-as-judge are the largest and most mature areas, with dedicated surveys; self-refinement is large but has turned sceptical since 2024; candidate selection is mature in MT and reasoning and comparatively thin for long-form prose; plan-conformance checking and two-model content/voice division of labour are the thinnest, with mostly indirect evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Self-correction: surveyed twice in TACL 2024, first as a taxonomy of automated-feedback methods and then as a critical review concluding that feedback generation is the bottleneck and that many positive results came from weak baselines or oracle information. — [Pan et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03188); [Kamoi et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297)
- Hallucination mitigation and long-form factuality: multiple surveys (from memory) and a steady stream of evaluation tooling through 2025 (VeriScore, VeriFastScore, FactReasoner, small-model decomposers), indicating the field's centre of gravity is detection and scoring rather than repair. — [Huang et al. 2023 survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.05232); [Tonmoy et al. 2024 survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01313); [VeriScore](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.19276); [VeriFastScore](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16973v1); [FactReasoner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18573)
- LLM-as-judge: at least two broad surveys in late 2024 plus a bias literature that continues into 2025-2026 (self-preference, correlated errors, best-of-N selection validity). — [Gu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594); [Li et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05579); [Kim et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)
- Text revision and editing models: an active line in 2022-2023 (IteraTeR, DELIteraTeR, PEER, EditEval, CoEdIT) largely overtaken by instruction-following LLMs; the 2025-2026 work studies what LLM editing does to text (LAMP edits, distortion and homogenisation, minimal-edit GEC, over-editing benchmarks) rather than proposing new editors. — [Raheja et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.09857); [Chakrabarty et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14509); [How LLMs Distort Our Written Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18161); [When Models Edit Too Much](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.04061)
- Candidate selection: MBR has been reframed as the general case covering self-consistency and reranking and extended to LLM-judge utilities (ICLR 2025); scaling studies are on code and math; compound-system and mixture-of-agents work shows quality of candidates matters more than diversity. — [Bertsch et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01387); [Wu et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02902); [Brown et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787); [Li et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674)
- Style, voice and "slop": a newer strand from 2024 onward combining expert-writer studies, detector-based measurement and decoding or fine-tuning fixes; mostly preprints. — [Chakrabarty et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13939); [Paech et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061); [Xu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19516)

### Inferences
- Trend, 2023 to 2026: from "the model can fix itself" (Self-Refine, Reflexion, debate) to "correction needs an external or stronger verifier and a decomposable check" (Huang, Kamoi, Xu), and from proposing editors to measuring the side effects of LLM editing (homogenisation, drift, over-editing).
- The requester's proposed pipeline sits at an intersection where each component has support but the composition does not: external strong verifier (supported), decomposable plan-item check (supported in principle, unevaluated as a loop), N-candidate selection (supported, plateauing, limited by checker recall and correlated errors), minimal repair by a different-register model (supported for content preservation, contradicted for voice preservation unless edits are tightly span-limited).
- The measured gains that are most transferable in size: one verify-and-repair pass buys roughly 5-15 points of factual precision; N of about 4-16 buys most of the selection gain; strong-model rewriting costs measurable register shift even under minimal-edit instructions.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain publication counts per area from a bibliometric source; the census above is a qualitative judgement from surveys and from what searches returned.
- Practitioner reports with measurements (blogs) were not found for this specific combination; the only non-academic sources surfaced were vendor posts on humanizers, which I did not use as evidence.
- Several 2026 items (edit-level voting, MECV minimal-edit correction, Plan-and-Refine, the best-of-N judge-validity paper) were seen only as search snippets and need to be read before being relied on.
