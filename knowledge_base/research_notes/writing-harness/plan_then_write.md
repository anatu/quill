# Plan-then-write generation: prior art census

Notes on method. Numbers below were read from the papers' arXiv/ar5iv full text unless marked "abstract only" or "secondary". Where a venue is marked "(venue from memory)", the fetched page did not state it and it should be checked before it is printed. "pp" means percentage points. All work is English unless noted.

## 1. Classical NLG: what the content-planning → sentence-planning → realisation pipeline established

### Takeaway
Classical NLG treated explanatory text as something to be planned before it is worded: a text planner chose and ordered content with typed rhetorical structure (schemas, then RST relations, then intention-plus-rhetoric plans), and later stages worded it. The same literature already named the pipeline's main weakness, the "generation gap": early planning decisions have unforeseen consequences downstream.

### Cited Findings
- The consensus pipeline has three modules: a text planner (content selection plus text structuring, "what to say"), a sentence planner or microplanner (aggregation, lexicalisation, referring expressions) and a linguistic realiser. Gatt and Krahmer describe it as a generalisation from practice that was claimed to be a de facto standard. — [Gatt & Krahmer 2018, JAIR 61:65–170](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1703.09902); [JAIR record](https://jair.org/index.php/jair/article/view/11173)
- Early text structuring used hand-written, domain-dependent rules that McKeown called schemata; schemata fix the content and order of clauses in a paragraph. — [Gatt & Krahmer 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1703.09902)
- Hovy (ACL 1988, "Planning Coherent Multisentential Text", pp. 163–169) operationalised RST relations (Mann and Thompson 1988) as plan operators to build coherent paragraphs; search results describe it as the first use of RST relations to control text construction. — [Hovy 1988, P88-1020](https://preview.aclanthology.org/moar-dois/P88-1020.pdf)
- Moore and Paris (Computational Linguistics 19(4):651–694, 1993) argued that explanation dialogues need a plan that records the intended effect of each text part on the hearer as well as the rhetorical relations between parts; schemata and purely rhetorical plans lack that intentional layer. Their case was follow-up questions in advisory/explanation dialogue: the system must know why it said something to re-explain it. — [Moore & Paris 1993, J93-4004](https://aclanthology.org/J93-4004/); [1989 ACL version, P89-1025](https://preview.aclanthology.org/fix_video/P89-1025.pdf)
- Known criticism: the "generation gap" is a mismatch between strategic (planning) and tactical (wording) components; "early decisions in the pipeline have unforeseen consequences further downstream", and constraints such as length are hard to enforce on pre-linguistic plans. Interactive, blackboard and revision architectures were proposed at a cost in efficiency. — [Gatt & Krahmer 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1703.09902)
- By 2018 the survey called integrated (non-modular) generation "now the dominant trend", while noting the strategic/tactical split still shapes data-driven work. — [Gatt & Krahmer 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1703.09902)

### Inferences
- The requester's design (typed moves bound to details, then a separate realiser) is structurally the classical text-plan-to-realiser split, with Moore-and-Paris-style typed plan nodes. The novelty is who fills each role (a strong LLM as planner, a small tuned model as realiser), not the architecture.
- The classical evidence is architectural and qualitative. I found no classical controlled comparison of planned versus unplanned explanatory text with effect sizes.

### Gaps
- Reiter and Dale's own texts were not fetched. From memory (unverified here): Reiter & Dale, "Building applied natural language generation systems", Natural Language Engineering 3(1), 1997, and the book Building Natural Language Generation Systems, CUP 2000, which name the stages document planning, microplanning and surface realisation. McKeown's Text Generation (CUP 1985) and Mann & Thompson, "Rhetorical Structure Theory", Text 8(3), 1988, are likewise cited from memory.
- Hovy's 1993 Artificial Intelligence article on discourse-structure relations was not retrieved.

## 2. Neural and LLM plan-then-write work, 2018–2026: per-paper census

### Takeaway
Across data-to-text, summarisation, stories and encyclopedic writing, an explicit plan stage reliably improves content fidelity, coverage and organisation over a matched no-plan baseline, typically by 10–20 pp on human pairwise coherence/relevance judgements or by cutting contradicted/omitted facts by half or more; fluency is usually unchanged, and n-gram overlap metrics sometimes fall. Most human studies are small (20–50 items) with low rater agreement.

### Cited Findings

Data-to-text (peer reviewed)
- Puduppully, Dong & Lapata, "Data-to-Text Generation with Content Selection and Planning" (AAAI 2019). Plan: ordered sequence of table records, predicted by a pointer network; plans for training extracted from reference summaries with an IE system (94% accuracy, about 80% relation recall). Realiser: encoder-decoder with copy. RotoWire test: relation-generation precision 74.80 → 87.47, content-selection recall 36.18 → 51.22, ordering (DLD) 15.42 → 18.58, BLEU 14.19 → 16.50 versus the no-plan WS-2017 baseline. Human (30 games, 3 raters): supported facts per 4 sentences 3.19 → 4.90, contradicted 1.09 → 0.90; best-worst coherence −4.89 → −2.44. — [arXiv 1809.00582](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.00582)
- Puduppully & Lapata, "Data-to-text Generation with Macro Planning" (TACL 2021). Plan: sequence of paragraph plans (entities/events per paragraph). RotoWire: RG precision 97.6 versus 81.3 for its own no-plan ablation; CS F1 42.9 versus 28.1; BLEU 15.46 versus 14.00 (but below other baselines at 16.5–17.2). MLB: RG precision 94.4 versus 92.7, BLEU 12.62 versus 11.09. Human (40 summaries, 3 raters): contradicted facts 0.27 versus 0.91 (ED+CC) on RotoWire and 0.25 versus 0.72 on MLB; MLB coherence 26.67 versus −12.08. Generated text adheres to its predicted plan at above 98% CS-F on RotoWire. — [arXiv 2102.02723](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.02723)
- Moryossef, Goldberg & Dagan, "Step-by-Step: Separating Planning from Realization in Neural Data-to-Text Generation" (NAACL 2019, venue from memory). Plan: symbolic ordered trees fixing sentence split, fact order and relation direction, chosen by a non-neural scorer; a neural MT model realises each sentence plan. WebNLG: BLEU 47.4 versus 46.5 for the end-to-end baseline. Manual faithfulness on 139 inputs (440 triples), single blinded annotator: omitted facts 41 → 6, wrong lexicalisations 39 → 17, over-generation 29 → 3. Crowd fluency on par (−0.6%). — [arXiv 1904.03396](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.03396)
- Castro Ferreira et al., "Neural data-to-text generation: A comparison between pipeline and end-to-end architectures" (EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019). Five-stage neural pipeline (ordering, structuring, lexicalisation, referring expressions, realisation). WebNLG BLEU all/seen/unseen: pipeline GRU 50.55/55.75/38.55 versus end-to-end GRU 33.49/57.20/6.25. Human 1–7 (223 samples, 35 raters): fluency 5.31 versus 4.73; semantics 5.21 versus 4.47. Share of trials with extra (hallucinated) content 0.01 versus 0.41; share verbalising all triples 0.67 versus 0.47. — [arXiv 1908.09022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.09022)

Summarisation and long-form QA (peer reviewed)
- Narayan et al., "Planning with Learned Entity Prompts for Abstractive Summarization" (FROST; TACL 2021, venue from memory). Plan: ordered entity chain generated before the summary by the same model. ROUGE roughly flat versus PEGASUS (XSum 47.56 → 47.80 R1). Planning alone did not raise faithfulness; pruning plan entities not found in the source ("drop") raised entity precision 0.357 → 0.746 and human faithfulness 3.31 → 3.53 on the hard XSum subset (50 documents per subset). — [arXiv 2104.07606](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.07606)
- Narayan et al., "Conditional Generation with a Question-Answering Blueprint" (TACL 2023, 11:974–996). Plan: ordered question-answer pairs, created for training by a question generator over the reference, and predicted at inference. Versus LongT5 with no plan: entailment (ANLI) 82.52 → 84.77 on AQuaMuSe, 56.11 → 64.37 on WikiCatSum, 8.40 → 20.84 on SummScreen-FD for the iterative variant, while ROUGE-L fell (61.43 → 56.48 on AQuaMuSe). Human ranking was mixed: LongT5 ranked best most often on AQuaMuSe. — [arXiv 2207.00397](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.00397); [TACL](https://transacl.org/index.php/tacl/article/view/4607)
- Fierro et al., "Learning to Plan and Generate Text with Citations" (ACL 2024). Plan: question blueprint; each sentence carries citations. AQuAMuSe AutoAIS 72.64 (no plan) → 74.16 (abstractive plan) → 74.35 (extractive plan); human fully-supported sentences 46.91% → 49.41% → 58.28% (50 instances). — [arXiv 2404.03381](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03381v3)

Stories and long narrative
- Yao et al., "Plan-and-Write: Towards Better Automatic Storytelling" (AAAI 2019, venue from memory). Plan: one keyword per sentence (storyline). Human pairwise, 300 titles, 5 judgements each: static plan versus no-plan LM, overall preference 40.9% versus 34.2%; fidelity 38.5% versus 16.3%; coherence 39.4% versus 32.3%. — [arXiv 1811.05701](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.05701)
- Rashkin et al., "PlotMachines" (EMNLP 2020). Input outline of plot phrases plus a tracked plot-state memory and discourse tags. Humans preferred it over fine-tuned GPT on outline use (80%) and transitions (80%), but not over Grover on outline use (25%); 20 stories per pair. — [arXiv 2004.14967](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.14967)
- Tan et al., "Progressive Generation of Long Text with Pretrained Language Models" (ProGen; NAACL 2021, venue from memory). Plan: keyword sketches at increasing vocabulary sizes, each stage a fine-tuned BART. Human (50 examples, 3 raters): passage coherence 2.95 (BART) → 3.83; sentence-level coherence 70.29% → 86.22%; fluency 4.59 → 4.46. — [arXiv 2006.15720](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.15720)
- Yang et al., "Re3" (EMNLP 2022, venue inferred from code URL). GPT-3 writes setting, characters and a three-point outline, then drafts. Versus rolling-window generation: coherent 45.7% → 60.0%, relevant 44.0% → 64.0%. Removing the plan module: coherent 63.3% → 46.7%, relevant 63.7% → 50.7%. — [arXiv 2210.06774](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.06774)
- Yang et al., "DOC: Improving Long Story Coherence With Detailed Outline Control" (ACL 2023). Depth-3 outline with setting and characters per node, plus a token-level controller. Versus Re3 (20 stories, 3 annotators): coherent 45.1% → 67.6%, relevant 37.1% → 65.3%, interesting 39.4% → 60.1%. Fleiss' kappa 0.15–0.24. — [arXiv 2212.10077](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10077)
- Huot et al., "Agents' Room" (ICLR 2025). Four planning agents and five writing agents with a shared scratchpad; expert evaluators preferred its stories to baselines (abstract only, no numbers retrieved). — [arXiv 2410.02603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02603)

Argument and opinion text
- Hua & Wang, "Sentence-Level Content Planning and Style Specification for Neural Text Generation" (EMNLP-IJCNLP 2019). Plan: per-sentence keyphrases plus a sentence-type label. Arguments: BLEU 3.64 (seq2seq) → 13.19; oracle plan 16.30. — [arXiv 1909.00734](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.00734)
- Hu et al., "PLANET" (ACL 2022). Latent sentence-level plan generated before each sentence; abstract reports significantly better coherence and content richness in automatic and human evaluation (numbers not retrieved). — [arXiv 2203.09100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.09100v1)

Expository, encyclopedic, scientific and educational text
- Balepur, Huang & Chang, "Expository Text Generation: Imitate, Retrieve, Paraphrase" (EMNLP 2023). The closest match to the requester's design: a GPT-2 Large "Imitator" writes a one-sentence stylistic content plan, a DistilBERT retriever fetches facts for that plan, and a BART paraphraser words the facts in the plan's style (about 1.2B parameters total). WikiCS: ROUGE-1 0.490 versus 0.169 for LLaMA-7B with retrieval; hallucinated-token rate 0.68% versus 4.51%. Ablation, replacing content plans with a topic query: ROUGE-1 0.490 → 0.165, hallucination 0.68 → 2.58, FactCC 0.616 → 0.329. Human (30 documents, 2 raters): 4.75 versus ChatGPT 4.41 on U.S. News, 4.66 versus 4.18 on Medline. — [arXiv 2305.03276](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.03276); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.729)
- Shao et al., "STORM" (NAACL 2024). Outline built from simulated multi-perspective research conversations, then section-by-section writing with citations. Removing the outline stage: ROUGE-1 45.82 → 26.77, entity recall 14.10 → 7.39, coverage 4.88 → 4.37 (LLM-judged rubric, 100 topics). Ten Wikipedia editors, 20 article pairs, versus an outline-driven RAG baseline: organisation 3.25 → 4.00 on a 1–7 scale (p = 0.005), other criteria not significant, verifiability 3.85 versus 3.80. — [arXiv 2402.14207](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.14207)
- Wang et al., "From Knowing to Teaching" (TeachCraft; ACL 2026). Lessons from multiple sources via three agents: classify sources by pedagogical intent, order objectives from foundational to advanced, generate. 67.8% win rate in human evaluation and 79.6% under an LLM judge against eight baselines on 40 expert lessons; ablations show each decision contributes (abstract only). — [ACL Anthology 2026.acl-long.1328](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1328/)

Preprints (not peer reviewed as far as the fetched pages show)
- Ning et al., "Skeleton-of-Thought" (ICLR 2024, venue from memory). Skeleton then parallel point expansion, aimed at latency: more than 2× speed-up on 8 of 12 models; quality under GPT-4 judging no worse in about 60% of cases, better on diversity and relevance, worse on coherence and immersion; no human evaluation. — [arXiv 2307.15337](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.15337)
- Liang et al., "Integrating Planning into Single-Turn Long-Form Text Generation" (Google DeepMind preprint, Oct 2024). A model fine-tuned to emit an LLM-synthesised summary/outline before the article in one pass: ROUGE-Lsum +2.47 on SciNews and +4.39 on Wikipedia over fine-tuning without the plan; human side-by-side win/loss 3.60 on SciNews and 1.56 on Wikipedia (50 items each). — [arXiv 2410.06203](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.06203v1)
- Wu et al., "LongEval" (preprint, 2025). Plan-based section-by-section writing versus direct generation on arXiv-paper generation, LLM-judged: GPT-4o overall 61 → 82. Length-following is the largest gap. Direct generation tested on one domain only. — [arXiv 2502.19103](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.19103v2)
- Xiong et al., "Beyond Outlining" (WriteHERE; preprint, 2025). Recursive typed planning (retrieve, reason, compose) interleaved with writing. LLM-judged story strength on 230 prompts with Claude 3.5 Sonnet: end-to-end 0.025, Agents' Room 0.694, WriteHERE 2.852. — [arXiv 2503.08275](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08275v3)
- Lee et al., "Navigating the Path of Writing" (preprint, 2024; Korean blog posts). Outline augmentation raised outline diversity; no numeric no-outline comparison in the text. — [arXiv 2404.13919](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.13919v1)
- Gu et al., "RAPID" (preprint 2025; a search summary lists it as ACL 2025 Findings, unverified). Retrieval-augmented outline plus plan-guided writing for encyclopedic articles; claims significant gains (abstract only). — [arXiv 2503.00751](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.00751)

### Inferences
- The largest and best-replicated effects are on content fidelity (what is said, and whether it matches the source), not on prose quality. Fluency is flat or slightly lower in every study that measured it (Moryossef −0.6%, ProGen 4.59 → 4.46).
- Gains from a plan shrink when the baseline is strong and already structured: STORM's only significant human gain over an outline-using baseline was organisation; DOC's large gains were over Re3, itself a planner.
- Only Balepur et al. and Liang et al. train a small or mid-size realiser on plans. Balepur et al. is the nearest precedent for "small tuned models realise a per-sentence plan for expository text", and it beat a prompted LLM with a system of about 1.2B parameters, but on short texts (73–90 words) with the plan produced by a small model, not a strong one.
- No retrieved paper pairs a strong LLM planner with a small fine-tuned realiser for multi-paragraph textbook prose. That specific division of labour appears to be open.

### Gaps
- Human-evaluation numbers for PLANET, Agents' Room, RAPID and TeachCraft were not retrieved (abstracts only).
- DYPLOC, PAIR, DiscoDVT, Fan et al.'s hierarchical story generation, Goldfarb-Tarrant et al.'s Aristotelian rescoring, Dramatron and LongWriter are known members of this family but were not fetched; no numbers are given for them here.
- I found no controlled study isolating outline-then-write from direct generation across several current frontier models with human raters.

## 3. Typed rhetorical moves as a control signal

### Takeaway
Typed sentence-level labels help when tested, but the direct evidence is thin: one peer-reviewed human study (Hua and Wang 2019) with a three-label set, plus ablations in RST- and discourse-tag-conditioned models. I found no study that conditions generation on an explanation-move inventory (motivate, define, example, contrast) and measures coherence or faithfulness.

### Cited Findings
- Hua & Wang (EMNLP 2019) add a sentence-type label to each sentence plan: Claim / Premise / Functional for arguments (labelled by rules), and length bins for Wikipedia. Removing the label, with keyphrase planning kept, lowered human ratings on arguments (30 samples, 3 raters, 1–5): grammaticality 3.99 → 3.03, correctness 2.78 → 2.26, content richness 2.61 → 2.03. BLEU fell 13.19 → 12.61; ROUGE-L was unchanged (20.15 versus 20.28). On Wikipedia only correctness was significant (3.24 versus 2.89). — [arXiv 1909.00734](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.00734)
- Adewoyin, Dutta & He, "RSTGen" (NAACL 2022) condition GPT-2 on an RST tree (relations, nuclearity, positions). Ablating RST relations on story generation: BLEU-1 24.01 → 22.28, Distinct-4 84.85 → 81.09. With gold trees, coherence scores (GRUEN) were about equal to baselines (−0.75 versus −0.76 for CTRL); with predicted trees they were worse (−0.86 to −0.88). No human evaluation numbers. — [arXiv 2205.12590](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.12590)
- PlotMachines uses paragraph discourse tags (introduction, body, conclusion). Removing them from the no-memory variant gave mixed ROUGE changes (Wikiplots R-L 15.5 → 13.9; WritingPrompts 23.5 → 23.2). — [arXiv 2004.14967](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.14967)
- Stevens-Guille et al. (SIGDIAL 2022): adding the intended PDTB relation to BART's input "significantly improves the consistency" with which the relation is correctly realised (abstract only, no numbers). — [ACL Anthology 2022.sigdial-1.48](https://aclanthology.org/2022.sigdial-1.48/)
- Xu, Li & Choi (ACL 2022) define six sentence-level functional roles for long-form answers and annotate 3.9k sentences in 640 answers; about half of sentences do something other than directly answer. This is analysis, not control, and the authors note textbook-style answers may need roles outside their ontology. — [ACL Anthology 2022.acl-long.249](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-long.249)
- Swales' CARS moves: retrieved work uses LLMs to detect moves, or shows that prompts naming the gap and significance moves yield introductions matching all three moves; none measures a quality gain from move-conditioned generation. — [prompt study](https://jurnal.uisu.ac.id/index.php/languageliteracy/article/download/11122/pdf); [CARS detection](https://research.edgehill.ac.uk/en/publications/automatic-detection-of-the-cars-framework-in-scholarly-writing-us/)
- Typed plan nodes of a different kind: WriteHERE's ablation removing task types (retrieve / reason / compose) dropped LLM-judged story strength from 2.143 to 0.717 with GPT-4o. — [arXiv 2503.08275](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08275v3)
- TeachCraft types source documents by pedagogical intent before sequencing; its ablations credit that step independently (abstract only). — [ACL Anthology 2026.acl-long.1328](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1328/)

### Inferences
- Typed labels add value beyond content planning in the one clean ablation, about 0.5–1.0 points on a 5-point human scale, with n = 30. That is suggestive, not established.
- Label quality at inference is the binding constraint: RSTGen's quality gain disappeared when trees were predicted. A strong LLM producing the move sequence is the obvious remedy, and it is untested in the retrieved literature.
- A typed explanation-move backbone for textbook prose looks like a real gap in the literature rather than a crowded area.

### Gaps
- No retrieved paper tests move-typed control for educational or expository explanations with a no-type baseline.
- Question-under-discussion plans and PDTB-conditioned LLM generation (2023–2026) returned no controlled results.
- The SciIG benchmark's arXiv page does not mention CARS; a search summary's claim that it uses CARS-style purposes is unverified. — [arXiv 2508.14273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14273)

## 4. Where the errors go: factual errors, mis-attribution, omission, incoherence

### Takeaway
Plans reduce omissions and unsupported content most, contradictions moderately, and long-range incoherence somewhat. The faithfulness gain comes from binding: when each plan element names the facts it covers, and the realiser is checked or constrained against it, unsupported content drops sharply; a plan that only orders topics does little for verifiability.

### Cited Findings
- Omission and over-generation fall most when facts are bound to sentence plans: omissions 41 → 6, over-generated facts 29 → 3, wrong lexicalisations 39 → 17 over 440 triples. — [Moryossef et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.03396)
- End-to-end models added content absent from the input in 41–53% of trials; pipelines in 1–8%. — [Castro Ferreira et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1908.09022)
- Contradicted facts per sample fell from 0.91 to 0.27 (RotoWire) and 0.72 to 0.25 (MLB) with paragraph-level plans. Residual errors were mis-attribution on long inputs: the wrong pitcher attached to a play, a percentage copied from the wrong team. — [Macro planning](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.02723)
- A plan by itself did not improve faithfulness in FROST; editing the plan to remove unsupported entities did (entity precision 0.357 → 0.746). The same edit lowered ROUGE and entity F1 when the reference legitimately needed inferred entities. — [FROST](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.07606)
- QA blueprints raised entailment by 2–12 points; the variant that plans and writes one sentence at a time was the most faithful and the most redundant (40–82% redundant QA pairs). — [QA blueprint](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.00397)
- Plans improved attribution: fully supported sentences 46.91% → 58.28% with extractive blueprints, where plan questions are copied from the source passages. — [Fierro et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.03381v3)
- For expository text, per-sentence plans used as retrieval queries cut hallucinated tokens from 2.58% to 0.68% and raised FactCC from 0.329 to 0.616 relative to a topic-level query; generating sentence by sentence rather than all at once cut hallucination 2.62% → 0.68%. Remaining errors came mostly from a competing fact in the retrieved set (for example an outdated ranking). — [Balepur et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.03276)
- An outline did not improve verifiability in STORM (3.80 versus 3.85). About 15% of sentences were unsupported, most often through improper inferential linking of facts, then inaccurate paraphrase. — [STORM](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.14207)
- In long stories, outlines raised coherence by 14–22 pp but long-range factual consistency (names, genders) remained a problem, and only 58.5% of low-level outline items were faithfully realised. — [DOC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10077); [Re3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.06774)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports the binding hypothesis fairly directly: the three strongest faithfulness results (Moryossef, Fierro's extractive blueprint, Balepur) all attach specific source facts to specific plan units, while the topic-outline systems (STORM, Re3) show no verifiability gain.
- The error type a bound plan does not fix is mis-attribution inside a unit, where the realiser picks the wrong one of several nearby facts. That is the residual error in Macro planning, Balepur and STORM alike, and it is the error an explanation realiser should be tested for.
- A plan gives a place to check and edit before any prose exists (FROST's drop step). That is a benefit of the harness, separate from the generation gain.

### Gaps
- No retrieved study measures mis-attribution of supporting details as its own category in multi-paragraph expository text.
- Faithfulness metrics differ across papers (entailment, FactCC, AutoAIS, human counts), so the effect sizes are not comparable across studies.

## 5. Failure modes reported

### Takeaway
The recurring failures are error propagation from wrong or noisy plans, plans that are ignored at fine granularity, lost cross-unit coherence when units are written independently, and rigidity. Reports of fluency loss are small. Direct evidence that over-detailed plans reduce generation to paraphrase was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Error propagation: storyline and story models trained separately let errors propagate; outline errors cascade into drafting; stage-wise models see imperfect inputs at test time. ProGen's remedy was to train on noised plans: without noise its distribution distance worsened from 39.94 to 47.18. — [Plan-and-Write](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.05701); [DOC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10077); [ProGen](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.15720)
- Plan quality is the ceiling: oracle plans beat predicted plans (argument BLEU 16.30 versus 13.19; Wikipedia 37.70 versus 33.76); ProGen with gold plans reached FBD-D 30.16 versus 39.94; predicted RST trees gave worse coherence than no RST. — [Hua & Wang](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.00734); [ProGen](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2006.15720); [RSTGen](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2205.12590)
- Noisy training plans: plans extracted automatically from references inherit extractor errors (about 80% recall in Puduppully 2019; noisy references produce noisy plans in FROST). — [Puduppully et al. 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.00582); [FROST](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2104.07606)
- Plans not followed: storyline words appeared in the story only 75–78% of the time; leaf-level outline relevance 58.5% in DOC; on unseen domains only 66.7% of sentence plans were realised with all entities. — [Plan-and-Write](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.05701); [DOC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10077); [Moryossef et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.03396)
- Independent expansion hurts coherence: Skeleton-of-Thought "currently ignores the dependencies between points", lowering coherence and immersion and failing on maths and code. — [Skeleton-of-Thought](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.15337)
- Stronger control costs variety: higher control strength in DOC gave repetitive, narrow text, and the no-control variant was rated more interesting than full DOC in one ablation (58.8 versus 50.0); outline leaves were sometimes too vague and sometimes over-expanded. — [DOC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10077)
- Fluency and naturalness: missing referring-expression handling left planned output 5.4% below human references; Hua and Wang's arguments overfit stylistic phrasing and rarely introduced new concepts. — [Moryossef et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.03396); [Hua & Wang](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1909.00734)
- Overlap metrics can fall while faithfulness rises (blueprints: ROUGE-L −5 on AQuaMuSe; Macro: BLEU below three baselines). — [QA blueprint](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2207.00397); [Macro planning](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.02723)
- Rigidity: "Current approaches rely on predefined workflows and rigid thinking patterns to generate outlines before writing"; a fixed pre-writing stage blocks adjustment when something is discovered mid-draft. A 2026 preprint makes the same criticism of static up-front outlines for very long outputs. — [WriteHERE](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.08275v3); [IS-Writer preprint, via search summary](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09709v1)
- Cost: DOC is two to three times slower than Re3; iterative per-sentence pipelines scale linearly in sentences. — [DOC](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.10077); [Balepur et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.03276)
- The classical name for the underlying problem is the generation gap. — [Gatt & Krahmer 2018](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1703.09902)

### Inferences
- For a strong-planner/small-realiser system the two risks most supported by evidence are (a) a train/test mismatch between the plans the realiser was trained on and the plans the planner emits, for which noise injection is the documented fix, and (b) silent non-realisation of plan items, which argues for a per-item coverage check after writing.
- Writing each move independently is the documented route to the coherence loss seen in Skeleton-of-Thought; the realiser should see neighbouring moves or prior text.

### Gaps
- "Plan too detailed, generation becomes paraphrase" is not measured in any paper retrieved; the nearest evidence is DOC's control-versus-interestingness trade-off and Hua and Wang's stylistic overfitting.
- No retrieved study measures homogenisation or loss of voice from outline stages.

## 6. Census, trend and surveys

### Takeaway
The approach has a prior art of at least a few hundred papers since 2018. Keyword counts suggest a steady 15–25 papers a year through 2021, a bump in 2022, and renewed growth in 2025–2026 as outline-first LLM writing agents multiplied, so the trend is rising rather than falling with stronger base models. I found no survey devoted to planning for text generation; coverage sits inside broader NLG surveys.

### Inferences
- The OpenAlex counts are a floor and a trend indicator, not a census: many core papers (STORM, Re3, DOC, Skeleton-of-Thought) do not use the searched phrases. A realistic total for 2018–2026 is several hundred papers; that estimate is mine.
- The centre of gravity moved twice: from data-to-text and summarisation with trained planners (2018–2022) to prompted LLM outline pipelines and agents (2023–2026). The evidence quality moved with it, from small human studies with matched baselines to LLM-judged comparisons run by the method's authors.
- In the LLM era the plan's measured benefit is concentrated in length, coverage and organisation of long outputs. Whether a plan improves paragraph-scale expository prose from a frontier model has not been shown with human raters.

### Gaps
- Google Scholar citation counts were not collected.
- No dedicated survey of planning in text generation was found in two searches; one may exist under a different title.
- No independent replication or meta-analysis of plan-versus-no-plan effects was found; nearly every comparison comes from the paper proposing the planner.
