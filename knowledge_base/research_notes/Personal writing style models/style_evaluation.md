# Evaluating "Does This Sound Like Me?": Methods for Measuring Personal Style Fidelity of Generated Text

Scope: methods a solo builder can run locally (Apple silicon M4 Pro, 24 GB unified memory) to score how closely generated text matches one person's writing. Research current to Nov 2026. Memory figures marked "inference" are estimated from parameter counts and were not measured.

## 1. Learned style representations (LUAR, Wegmann/CISR, StyleDistance, mStyleDistance, others)

### Takeaway
All the major style-embedding models are small (82M to about 125M parameters), on Hugging Face, and easy to run on CPU or MPS. They differ a lot in how well they ignore content. LUAR is the strongest authorship-ID model, but at sentence level it mostly tracks topic (STEL-or-Content 0.03). StyleDistance has the best content-independence (S-o-C 0.29). Wegmann/CISR is close behind (0.22). None is reliable on its own for telling "personalized LLM output" apart from "generic LLM output".

### Cited Findings
**LUAR (Rivera-Soto et al., EMNLP 2021, "Learning Universal Authorship Representations")**
- `rrivera1849/LUAR-MUD`: 82.5M params, F32 safetensors, 512-dim output, Apache-2.0. It needs `trust_remote_code=True`. Input is an "episode" of several texts per author, shaped (batch × episode_length × tokens). The card examples use max_length 32 and 512. It was trained on the Reddit Million User Dataset (MUD). — [HF: LUAR-MUD](https://huggingface.co/rrivera1849/LUAR-MUD)
- `rrivera1849/LUAR-CRUD` was trained on a Pushshift Reddit subsample: 2015–2019 comments from about 5M users with at least 100 comments each. — [HF: LUAR-CRUD](https://huggingface.co/rrivera1849/LUAR-CRUD)
- `rrivera1849/LUAR-MUD-MV-Qwen` is a newer multi-vector successor. It reports MUD test MRR 0.6564, against 0.5706 for the released LUAR-MUD (0.6283 in single-vector mode). — [HF: LUAR-MUD-MV-Qwen](https://huggingface.co/rrivera1849/LUAR-MUD-MV-Qwen)
- Also from the same author: `rrivera1849/LUSR`, a style representation trained without authorship labels and aimed mainly at machine-text detection. A community port, `gabrielloiseau/LUAR-MUD-sentence-transformers`, exists. The code is at github.com/llnl/luar (Apache-2.0). — [HF search results / LLNL LUAR](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fllnl%2Fluar); [HF: LUAR-MUD-sentence-transformers](https://huggingface.co/gabrielloiseau/LUAR-MUD-sentence-transformers)
- Content-independence at sentence level (StyleDistance paper, Table 3, averaged over 5 features): LUAR scores STEL 0.86 and STEL-or-Content 0.03. Plain roberta-base scores 0.90 and 0.05. So a high STEL score alone is confounded by content. — [StyleDistance paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12757)
- In a 2026 UMD user study (n=81), LUAR cosine similarity was chosen because it "performed best on authorship identification using their control texts". Participants' own self-similarity ratings correlated only weakly with LUAR (r ≈ 0.24). — [Baumler et al. 2026, arXiv 2604.24444](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444)

**Wegmann et al. 2022 ("Same Author or Just Same Topic? Towards Content-Independent Style Representations", RepL4NLP 2022)**
- `AnnaWegmann/Style-Embedding` is RoBERTa-base and outputs 768-dim vectors. The author calls it CISR (Content-Independent Style Representations). It was trained with conversation-based content control on Reddit; the paper reports that conversation control beats domain control or no control. Code: github.com/nlpsoc/style-embeddings. Evaluation uses the STEL framework (Wegmann & Nguyen, EMNLP 2021). — [HF: AnnaWegmann/Style-Embedding](https://huggingface.co/AnnaWegmann/Style-Embedding); [arXiv 2204.04907](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2204.04907)
- Results reported in the StyleDistance paper: STEL 0.77, STEL-or-Content 0.22, and PAN 2011–15 authorship-verification ROC-AUC 0.56 on average (0.65 / 0.39 / 0.57 / 0.63). — [StyleDistance paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12757)

**StyleDistance (Patel, Zhu, Qiu, Horvitz, Apidianaki, McKeown, Callison-Burch; arXiv 2410.12757, Oct 2024)**
- `StyleDistance/styledistance` is a fine-tune of FacebookAI/roberta-base: 0.1B params, BF16, MIT license, loadable with `SentenceTransformer('StyleDistance/styledistance')`. Training used SynthSTEL plus a real Reddit authorship dataset. A synthetic-only variant is `StyleDistance/styledistance_synthetic_only`. — [HF: StyleDistance](https://huggingface.co/StyleDistance/styledistance)
- SynthSTEL covers 40 style features in 7 categories, with 100 positive/negative pairs per feature, and was generated by GPT-4 through DataDreamer. About 320K training triplets were sampled from it. Human validation found 92% of judgments matched the intended label, with Krippendorff's α = 0.55. Content similarity between parallel examples is 0.88. — [StyleDistance paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12757)
- Results: STEL 0.87 and S-o-C 0.29, the best S-o-C of the real-data models. PAN 2011–15 AV ROC-AUC averages 0.64 (0.62 / 0.68 / 0.58 / 0.67), against LISA at 0.57 and Wegmann at 0.56. On a style-transfer-evaluation accuracy task: StyleDistance 0.46, Wegmann 0.39, LUAR 0.38, humans 0.37. — [StyleDistance paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12757)
- On unseen features, the synthetic-only model keeps 65% of its performance out-of-domain and 50% out-of-distribution. Stated limitations: the 40 features do not cover all styles, the synthetic data inherits LLM biases, the work is limited to sentence-level text, and some features overlap with content. — [StyleDistance paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12757)

**mStyleDistance (Qiu, Zhu, Patel, Apidianaki, Callison-Burch; arXiv 2502.15168)**
- `StyleDistance/mstyledistance` is a multilingual, content-independent style embedding trained on mSynthSTEL (about 40 style features across 9 non-English languages). It loads with sentence-transformers. The paper reports it beats existing models on multilingual style benchmarks and generalizes to unseen features and languages. — [HF: mstyledistance](https://huggingface.co/StyleDistance/mstyledistance); [arXiv 2502.15168](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.15168)

**Style embeddings as "write like me" evaluators (Jangra et al., "Evaluating Style-Personalized Text Generation", EMNLP 2026 main)**
- Benchmark: 8 datasets (Amazon reviews, ArXiv, blogs, Enron, lyrics, Reddit, Reuters, short stories), 636 instances, with about 500 reference tokens. Each instance is a pairwise choice of which candidate is closer in style to the reference. The settings are domain discrimination (DD), authorship attribution (AA), and personalized-vs-non-personalized LLM output (LLM).
- Accuracy (DD / AA / LLM / mean): Wegmann 0.910 / 0.670 / 0.558 / 0.713; StyleDistance 0.926 / 0.665 / 0.575 / 0.722; BLEU 0.869 / 0.700 / 0.631 / 0.733.
- Style embeddings degraded most from DD to LLM (−38.3%), against −26.7% for n-gram metrics and −11.3% for LLM judges. LUAR was not tested. — [arXiv 2508.06374v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06374v4)

### Inferences
- For a personal fidelity score, the most practical setup is the centroid of the user's held-out texts in StyleDistance or Wegmann space (content-robust), plus LUAR on multi-text episodes (strong authorship signal, but topic-leaky). Report both, and treat LUAR gains with suspicion when topic overlaps.
- All three models are about 0.1B parameters (about 0.3–0.5 GB in fp32, inference). They run comfortably on CPU or the `mps` device on an M4 Pro, and sentence-transformers supports `device="mps"`. LUAR's custom code may need a CPU fallback if an op is unsupported on MPS; this is untested.
- StyleDistance was trained at sentence level. For paragraph or document outputs, embed per sentence or per paragraph and average, or prefer LUAR episodes for long text.
- Before trusting any embedding for a given user, run a quick sanity check on their own data. It should rank held-out real texts by the user above texts by other authors on the same topic, as Baumler et al. did when they selected LUAR.

### Gaps
- No size or base model was confirmed for mStyleDistance; the model card did not show it in the fetched content.
- No AV/PAN or STEL numbers were found for LUAR-MUD-MV-Qwen or LUSR. LUAR was not included in the Jangra et al. evaluator comparison.
- No published benchmark directly measured MPS speed or compatibility for LUAR's custom code.

## 2. Authorship verification / attribution as an imitation metric (TinyStyler, STYLL, "Catch Me If You Can")

### Takeaway
Imitation papers score outputs with an AV or AA model trained on the target author's real texts: "would a verifier accept this as the author's?" The setups are reproducible locally with small models. LLM imitations of informal personal writing (blogs, forums) fail these tests badly even when they pass for news and email.

### Cited Findings
- **"Catch Me If You Can? Not Yet" (Wang, Tripto, Park, Li, Zhou; Findings of EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2509.14543)**
  - Setup:
    - AA uses fine-tuned Longformer-base-4096 and ModernBERT-base classifiers (20 epochs, lr 2e-5), reported as top-5 accuracy averaged over both.
    - AV uses the same two backbones fine-tuned on label-balanced pairs (4:6 positive:negative, 10 epochs), reported as top-1 accuracy.
    - Style matching builds a per-author model from LIWC-22 and WritePrints features. A text counts as correct if its Mahalanobis distance is closer to its true author's model than to other authors'.
    - AI detection uses GPTZero.
    - Semantic similarity uses METEOR, ROUGE-L, and SBERT.
  - Data: Enron (150 authors), Blog (100), CCAT50 (50), Reddit (100). Generation used 5-shot same-author examples plus a GPT-4.1 summary of the target, at temperature 0. — [arXiv 2509.14543 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)
  - Results:
    - 5-shot AV accuracy is high on news and email: CCAT50 95–97%, Enron 95.6–96.6%. It drops on informal text: Reddit 50–66%, Blog only 16.7–21.3% (GPT-4o 19.37, Gemini-2.0-Flash 21.25, Gemma-3-27B 16.72).
    - Top-5 AA on Blog is 38–44% and on Reddit 26–36%.
    - GPTZero labeled almost all generations as AI. GPT-4o was 0.08–0.67% "human" except Enron (16.86%). Gemma-3-27B reached at most 43.63% on Enron.
    - Going from 2 to 10 examples changed little. Topic-matched exemplars *reduced* attribution. — [arXiv 2509.14543 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)
  - Noted weaknesses: no large human evaluation; within-genre only; few samples per author for AA/AV training; zero-shot AA is above chance because of topic overlap, so AA does not isolate style; GPTZero may be biased toward GPT-family outputs. Code: github.com/jaaack-wang/llms-implicit-writing-styles-imitation. — [arXiv 2509.14543 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)
- **TinyStyler (Horvitz et al. 2024, arXiv 2406.15586)**
  - Authorship style transfer uses the Patel et al. (2022) STYLL splits: 15 source users × 15 target users, 16 samples per target, 3,600 transformations per split.
  - Style is scored by **Away** and **Towards**, computed with *held-out* UAR/LUAR embeddings. Wegmann embeddings are used only in training and reranking, which keeps the evaluator separate from the training signal.
  - Meaning is scored by Mutual Implication Score (MIS). Joint scores are reported.
  - There was no human evaluation for authorship ("difficult for humans to evaluate"). The model is about 800M params (T5-v1.1-Large), released as `tinystyler/tinystyler`. — [TinyStyler arXiv 2406.15586](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15586)
  - Joint scores (Random / Single / Diverse): STYLL-GPT-3 0.23 / 0.29 / 0.30; STYLL-BLOOM 0.34 / 0.40 / 0.36; GPT-3.5 0.35 / 0.41 / 0.37; GPT-4 0.33 / 0.31 / 0.30; TinyStyler 0.40 / 0.45 / 0.36; TinyStyler rerank(5) 0.43 / 0.48 / 0.39. — [TinyStyler arXiv 2406.15586](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15586)
- **Training-free verifier (Jemama & Kumar, IEEE UEMCON 2025, arXiv 2509.24930)**
  - Method: cosine distance over TF-IDF character 3–5-grams (top 10k) plus all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings, with no training.
  - Results: 97.49% accuracy (ROC-AUC 0.997) same-domain and 94.48% (AUC 0.981) cross-domain on student essays. Imitation pass rates were about 3–7% zero-shot (style profile only), 68–95% one-shot, 91–100% few-shot, and 97–100% completion.
  - Human essays average perplexity about 29.5 versus about 15–16 for LLM imitations. The authors conclude that style fidelity and detectability are separable. Code: github.com/rajeshjnu2006/writing-style-uemcon2025. — [arXiv 2509.24930](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24930v1)

### Inferences
- A local version of this protocol could be:
  1. Collect N user texts plus an "impostor" pool of other authors in the same genre (public Reddit, blog, or email corpora).
  2. Hold out 20% of the user texts.
  3. Train or calibrate a verifier. This can be a cosine threshold on StyleDistance or LUAR embeddings, char-n-gram TF-IDF, or a fine-tuned ModernBERT-base, which is small enough to fine-tune on MPS.
  4. Report the verifier's accept rate on generated outputs, alongside its accept rate on held-out real texts as a ceiling and on impostor texts as a floor.
- Following TinyStyler, keep the evaluator embedding different from any embedding used in training, reranking, or best-of-n selection. Otherwise the generator learns to game the metric.
- The large gap between Blog AV (about 20%) and email/news AV (about 96%) in "Catch Me" suggests informal personal writing is the hard case. Personal fidelity scores should be reported per genre.

### Gaps
- The STYLL paper (Patel et al. 2022, "Low-Resource Authorship Style Transfer") was not fetched directly. Its numbers above come via the TinyStyler reproduction.
- The "Catch Me" paper does not report decision thresholds for its AV models.

## 3. Classical stylometry features (function words, Burrows' Delta, sentence length, punctuation, TTR, readability; faststylometry, stylo, writeprints)

### Takeaway
Classical stylometry is cheap, interpretable, and runs anywhere. It is good at aggregate drift detection, for example "this batch of outputs uses far fewer em-dashes and longer sentences than my corpus". Delta-style attribution, however, needs thousands of words, so it cannot score a single short output reliably.

### Cited Findings
- **faststylometry** (Fast Data Science, Python, pip `faststylometry`) computes Burrows' Delta from function-word frequencies. It can estimate an authorship probability by calibrating on known same-author and different-author Delta values (`calibrate(train_corpus)`), and it can segment texts (`corpus.split(1000)`) to handle unequal lengths. The tutorial notes the two-class task is easy because the model "has learnt from entire books". — [Fast Data Science](https://fastdatascience.com/fast-stylometry-python-library); [PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/faststylometry)
- **Sample size (Eder):** In "Does Size Matter? Authorship Attribution, Small Samples, Big Problem" (DH 2010; LLC 2013, doi 10.1093/llc/fqt066), samples under about 5,000 words gave "poor guessing" because of random noise. Eder's later "Short Samples" work lowered the minimum to under 2,000 words, and his methodology page says the earlier findings were "(partially) falsified". A Cosine Delta study on Estonian fiction reports stable accuracy only at 5,000 words or more. — [DH2010 abstract](https://dh2010.cch.kcl.ac.uk/academic-programme/abstracts/papers/html/ab-688.html); [Eder methodology page (computationalstylistics)](https://computationalstylistics.github.io/projects/methodology); [Short Samples in AA](https://dh-abstracts.library.virginia.edu/works/3978)
- **WritePrints and LIWC-22** were the style-matching features in "Catch Me If You Can", combined with per-author Mahalanobis distance. — [arXiv 2509.14543](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)
- **EQ-Bench slop-score leaderboard** computes classical features as well: n-gram repetition, MATTR-500 (moving-average type-token ratio), Flesch-Kincaid grade, and dialogue frequency. — [sam-paech/slop-score](https://github.com/sam-paech/slop-score)
- In Jangra et al. (2026), plain n-gram overlap against the reference (BLEU) beat both style embeddings on mean accuracy (0.733 vs 0.722 / 0.713) and on the LLM setting (0.631 vs 0.575 / 0.558). The authors caution that n-gram metrics may be inflated by data contamination. — [arXiv 2508.06374v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06374v4)

### Inferences
- A practical "style fingerprint" diff could have per-feature z-scores of output against user corpus. The features would be: function-word profile (top 100–300 MFW), sentence-length mean and SD, punctuation rates per 1k chars (em-dash, semicolon, colon, parentheses, exclamation, ellipsis), contraction rate, paragraph length, MATTR, readability, emoji and capitalization habits. Each output then gets an explainable diff ("you use 3× fewer semicolons"). This is the most actionable signal for prompt or fine-tune iteration.
- Delta on outputs should be computed by pooling generations to 2,000+ words per condition and comparing pooled Delta to the user against pooled Delta to impostors.
- The R package `stylo` (Eder, Rybicki, Kestemont) is the reference implementation for Delta variants, clustering, and bootstrap consensus trees. faststylometry is the lighter Python option.

### Gaps
- The `stylo` R documentation and the original Writeprints paper (Abbasi & Chen 2008) were not fetched, so feature-set details are not verified here.
- No study was found validating a composite classical-feature score against human judgments of *personal* style fidelity.
- faststylometry publishes no minimum text length.

## 4. LLM-as-judge for style similarity

### Takeaway
Frontier LLM judges (gpt-4.1, o4-mini) were the strongest single pairwise style discriminators in the only systematic study found. **Small local judges (Llama-3.1-8B, Ministral-3B, Mistral-24B) scored at or below chance.** For a 24 GB Mac, a local LLM judge is the least trustworthy component unless it is validated on the user's own data.

### Cited Findings
- **Jangra et al., "Evaluating Style-Personalized Text Generation: Challenges and Directions" (arXiv 2508.06374, EMNLP 2026 main)**
  - Protocol: pairwise, "which candidate is closer in style to the reference?". Mean accuracy across DD / AA / LLM:
    - gpt-4.1: 0.815 (0.961 / 0.807 / 0.678)
    - o4-mini: 0.755
    - DeepSeek-V3: 0.658
    - Qwen3-32B: 0.607
    - Mistral-24B: 0.467
    - Ministral-3B: 0.324
    - Llama-3.1-8B: 0.279 (random baseline 0.500)
  - Smaller models often answered "Both" or "None" instead of choosing. — [arXiv 2508.06374v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06374v4)
  - Prompt sensitivity (Llama-3.1-8B): a structured-output prompt was about 24% worse than the original, and a binary-choice prompt about 34.5% better. Automatic prompt optimization gains were inconsistent and possibly overfit. — [arXiv 2508.06374v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06374v4)
  - Ensembles: the best ensemble of all evaluators with performance-weighted voting reached 0.821 mean, but these are oracle-style weights and not significantly better than gpt-4.1 alone. The best non-LLM ensemble (BLEU, ROUGE, METEOR, Wegmann, StyleDistance) reached 0.780, a 6.4% relative gain over BLEU. — [arXiv 2508.06374v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06374v4)
  - Human agreement ratios (Appendix E): content 0.779, **style 0.641**, copy-editing 0.400. Style preferences are more subjective than content preferences. — [arXiv 2508.06374v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06374v4)
- Expert-vs-lay human judges of style fidelity (blind pairwise study, arXiv 2601.18353):
  - Expert MFA writers had inter-annotator κ of 0.54–0.58 on stylistic fidelity. Lay Prolific judges had κ of 0.07–0.22.
  - On in-context imitations, experts and lay judges differed significantly: 72.0% vs 52.7% picked the human text as more faithful. — [arXiv 2601.18353](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18353v1)
- In a separate bias study, four of five LLM judges preferred markdown well above the human baseline. Judges can carry stylistic preferences humans do not share. — [arXiv 2604.23178](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23178)

### Inferences
- Use LLM judges **pairwise** (A vs B against reference excerpts), with forced binary choice and position swapping (run both orders, count only consistent wins). This follows the binary-choice finding and the known position-bias literature.
- Treat an absolute 1–10 "sounds like me" score as low-reliability. No study found validated absolute style scores against humans.
- On a 24 GB M4 Pro, a 4-bit Qwen3-32B-class model (about 18–20 GB, inference) is borderline and slow. 8B-class models scored below chance in the published benchmark. A cloud frontier judge is more defensible if privacy allows. Otherwise, calibrate any local judge on known pairs (user's real text vs other author, same topic) before using it.
- LLM judges are themselves LLMs. They may prefer polished, "LLM-ish" text over the user's real idiosyncrasies (self-preference/sycophancy risk). No study confirmed this specifically for personal style.

### Gaps
- No study was found measuring LLM-judge agreement with the *author's own* judgment of "sounds like me".
- No study was found on self-preference bias specifically in style matching.
- A search snippet claimed a fixed style-embedding baseline beat a GPT-4o judge on GPT continuations (82% vs 68%) but lost on LLaMA continuations (85% vs 81%), not significant. The underlying paper could not be identified or verified, so this is unconfirmed.
- No published local-judge (e.g., Qwen3-32B 4-bit via MLX) accuracy for this task on Apple silicon was found.

## 5. Detecting "AI-sounding" text / slop as a negative signal

### Takeaway
Slop lists and zero-shot detectors are useful *regression checks*: "did my fine-tune or prompt reintroduce LLM tics?" They are not fidelity metrics. Imitations can pass authorship verification while still being flagged as AI (low perplexity, GPTZero near 0% human), so the two signals should be tracked separately.

### Cited Findings
- **slop-score (sam-paech, MIT)**
  - Matches text against precomputed lists of words and trigrams over-represented in LLM text relative to a human baseline (wordfreq-derived frequencies). It also runs a two-stage "not X, but Y" detector: 10 surface regexes plus 35 POS-tagged patterns via wink-pos-tagger.
  - Composite weights: slop words 60%, not-X-but-Y 25%, slop trigrams 15%. The lists are in the repo's `data/` folder. It runs in the browser (`python -m http.server`) or hosted at eqbench.com/slop-score.html, and accepts custom human baselines (`--force-recalc-human`).
  - Validation against human judgments and the exact score normalization are not documented. — [github.com/sam-paech/slop-score](https://github.com/sam-paech/slop-score)
  - A third-party Go port states it is "not an AI-authorship detector and not a measure of writing quality" and may cover technical text less well. — [SlopScoreTool (pkg.go.dev)](https://pkg.go.dev/github.com/lemon07r/SlopScoreTool)
- **Antislop paper (Paech, Roush, Goldfeder, Shwartz-Ziv; arXiv 2510.15061, ICLR 2026 poster)**
  - Some slop patterns occur more than 1,000× more often in LLM output than in human text.
  - The Antislop Sampler backtracks on banned strings and can suppress 8,000+ patterns. Plain token banning becomes unusable at about 2,000.
  - FTPO (Final Token Preference Optimization) reportedly gives 85–90% suppression (gemma-3-12b; secondary sources say 85–92%) with minimal quality loss. DPO degraded quality and lexical diversity.
  - Code is MIT-licensed at sam-paech/auto-antislop. — [arXiv 2510.15061](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15061); [ICLR 2026 poster](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10008156)
- **EQ-Bench Creative Writing** has a Slop column and a "GPT-Slop Control" slider that penalizes overused LLM words and phrases. — [EQ-Bench creative writing](https://eqbench.com/creative_writing_v2.html)
- **Binoculars (Hans et al., arXiv 2401.12070)**
  - Zero-shot detector based on the ratio of perplexity to cross-perplexity between two LLMs. It "detects over 90% of generated samples from ChatGPT (and other LLMs) at a false positive rate of 0.01%", with no training data. — [arXiv 2401.12070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12070)
  - The default pair is Falcon-7B + Falcon-7B-Instruct, with a fixed global threshold tuned for that pair. Other model pairs can be passed in. The page says it is stronger for English than other languages and advises against using it without human supervision. License BSD-3. — [github.com/ahans30/Binoculars](https://github.com/ahans30/Binoculars)
  - Sources conflict on which Falcon model is observer and which is performer. — [HF blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/dmicz/binoculars-text-detection) vs [Weaviate summary](https://weaviate.io/papers/paper24)
- **Fast-DetectGPT (Bao et al., arXiv 2310.05130)**: the local demo defaults to gpt-neo-2.7B, and `--sampling_model_name gpt-j-6B` gives higher accuracy. Average AUROC is about 0.9887 white-box, against 0.9554 for DetectGPT (secondary summary). — [arXiv 2310.05130](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.05130); [repo summary](https://www.sourcepulse.org/projects/1843956)
- **Perplexity gap:** human essays average about 29.5 perplexity versus about 15–16 for LLM imitations (GPT-2 scorer). At a threshold of 20, about 90% of AI texts fall below it versus about 15% of human texts, even when imitations passed the style verifier. — [arXiv 2509.24930](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.24930v1)
- **GPTZero on imitations:** GPT-4o 5-shot imitations were "human" 0.08–0.67% of the time outside email. — [arXiv 2509.14543](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)
- **Pangram** was used alongside LUAR in the UMD post-editing study. Post-editing moved text away from the LLM draft on both measures, but residual LLM style remained. — [arXiv 2604.24444](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444)

### Inferences
- Binoculars with two 7B models in bf16 is about 28 GB of weights (inference), which does not fit in 24 GB unified memory. Options are 8-bit or 4-bit quantization (this shifts the calibrated threshold), a smaller same-family pair (e.g., a 1.5B–3B base/instruct pair, recalibrated), or Fast-DetectGPT with gpt-neo-2.7B (about 5–11 GB, inference).
- Calibrate any detector threshold on the user's *own* writing. Some human writers naturally score "AI-like", so the false positive rate on the user's corpus is the key number.
- A simple local slop check could count hits from the slop-score word and trigram lists, plus a "not X, but Y" regex, per 1k words. Compare against the user's own baseline rate, not zero, since the user may legitimately say "delve". Track it as a regression metric across model versions.
- Commercial detectors (GPTZero, Pangram) are API-only and cannot run locally. This is inferred; local availability was not checked.

### Gaps
- No published list of the top slop words with frequencies was fetched (the lists live in the repo's `data/` folder).
- No study validating slop-score against human "sounds AI" judgments was found.
- No published Binoculars results with small (<3B) model pairs or on Apple silicon were found.

## 6. Content preservation / meaning metrics to pair with style metrics

### Takeaway
Style-transfer papers pair a style score with a meaning score and report a joint or geometric-mean metric, so a model cannot "win" style by ignoring the task. MIS, an NLI-based bidirectional-entailment score, is the standard choice in authorship transfer (TinyStyler, STYLL). SBERT cosine is the lightweight alternative.

### Cited Findings
- **Mutual Implication Score (Babakov et al., ACL 2022 SRW)**: `s-nlp/Mutual_Implication_Score` (also under `SkolkovoInstitute/`) is a symmetric similarity score from a RoBERTa-large NLI model fine-tuned on QQP paraphrases. It outperforms all other measures on paraphrase detection and is on par with the best measures for style transfer. — [HF: s-nlp/Mutual_Implication_Score](https://huggingface.co/s-nlp/Mutual_Implication_Score); [ACL 2022 SRW paper](https://aclanthology.org/2022.acl-srw.23.pdf)
- A third-party listing gives the MIS license as CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; verify on the card before commercial use. — [PromptLayer](https://www.promptlayer.com/models/mutualimplicationscore)
- TinyStyler uses MIS for meaning preservation. Reranking uses the geometric mean of Away, Towards, and Sim, and formality Joint is the geometric mean of accuracy, Sim, and fluency (a CoLA-trained classifier). — [TinyStyler](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15586)
- "Catch Me If You Can" reports METEOR (about 0.30–0.32), ROUGE-L (about 0.23–0.26), and SBERT (about 0.75–0.78) between imitation and target, with the SBERT variant unspecified. — [arXiv 2509.14543](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.14543v1)
- An MIS-style bidirectional NLI content score was reported among the highest correlations with human judgments in formality transfer. — [arXiv 2308.09055](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.09055)

### Inferences
- A local recipe could be:
  1. Content = MIS(source brief or reference, output), or SBERT cosine (`all-MiniLM-L6-v2` or `all-mpnet-base-v2`).
  2. Style = StyleDistance or LUAR similarity to the user centroid, minus similarity to a generic-LLM centroid.
  3. Report a geometric mean, so collapse on either axis tanks the score.
- MIS is RoBERTa-large (about 355M params, about 1.4 GB fp32, inference) and runs fine on CPU or MPS.
- For open-ended generation with no source text (e.g., "write a post about X"), content preservation becomes "task adherence". There, an NLI check that required facts or bullet points are entailed by the output, or an LLM rubric check, is more appropriate than similarity to a reference.

### Gaps
- No BERTScore-vs-MIS comparison specific to personal-style tasks was fetched. BERTScore appears in Jangra et al. only as a descriptive statistic.
- No validated "task adherence" metric for open-ended personalized writing was found.

## 7. Practical blind-test protocols

### Takeaway
No study was found where people try to pick their *own* writing out of a pair with an LLM imitation. Related evidence suggests (a) self-judgment is a weak signal: authors rate LLM-tinged text as authentic, and self-ratings correlate with LUAR at only r ≈ 0.24; (b) expert or familiar readers discriminate far better than lay readers. Blind tests should be designed with these effects in mind.

### Cited Findings
- **Baumler et al. 2026 ("Can You Make It Sound Like You? Post-Editing LLM-Generated Text for Personal Style", UMD, arXiv 2604.24444)**
  - Design: n=81 Prolific participants on six personal tasks (e.g., wedding vows, apology letters), each done both unassisted and by post-editing an o4-mini draft.
  - Post-edited text moved toward the participant's style on LUAR and Pangram, but stayed closer to the LLM draft than to their own control writing. Post-edited texts were more homogeneous across people.
  - Participants rated post-edited text as just as representative of their style as unassisted writing, and self-ratings correlated weakly with LUAR (r ≈ 0.24). — [arXiv 2604.24444](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24444)
- **Expert vs LLM imitation of acclaimed authors (arXiv 2601.18353)**
  - Protocol:
    - Blind pairwise comparisons, with no disclosure of which text is AI.
    - Stylistic fidelity: judges see the original author's excerpt alongside a human-written and an AI-written imitation.
    - Each pair is judged by 3 experts and 5 lay judges (majority vote), with 2–5-sentence rationales.
    - Raters: 28 MFA writers and 131 Prolific readers. Items: 150 in-context pairs (GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, Gemini 1.5 Pro) and 90 fine-tuned pairs (GPT-4o, 30 authors).
  - Results: on stylistic fidelity, experts chose the human imitation 72.0% of the time against in-context LLMs. Against *fine-tuned* GPT-4o, experts chose the AI 81.1% and lay readers 80.0%. On writing quality, experts preferred human text 82.7% vs in-context, and preferred fine-tuned AI 62.2%. — [arXiv 2601.18353](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18353v1)
  - Expert κ was 0.54–0.58 on fidelity, against lay κ of 0.07–0.22. — [arXiv 2601.18353](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.18353v1)
- People's sense of ownership does not always match declared authorship. Some participants put their name on pseudo-personalized LLM text they neither wrote nor felt they owned. — [LMU "AI ghostwriter effect" (ACM TOCHI)](https://www.lmu.de/en/newsroom/news-overview/news/who-wrote-it-the-ai-ghostwriter-effect-80ece661.html)
- Human style-preference agreement is only moderate (0.641 agreement ratio). — [arXiv 2508.06374v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06374v4)

### Inferences
- **Suggested self-blind 2AFC protocol** (design synthesized from the studies above, not itself validated):
  1. Generate an output from a prompt derived from one of your held-out real texts (same brief, same length).
  2. Strip formatting cues and normalize length, quotes, and whitespace.
  3. Present the pair in random order and ask "which did you write?". Delay the test by days so you do not recognize content from memory.
  4. Run 20+ trials. Report the "fooled" rate: 50% means indistinguishable, and a one-sided binomial test at n=20 needs ≤ 6 of 20 correct for "fails to detect" or ≥ 15 of 20 for "reliably detects".
  5. Ask for a one-line reason each time. The reasons double as a tic list to feed back into the classical-feature and slop checks.
- Add a **third-party arm**: someone who knows your writing (a partner, colleague, or editor) does the same 2AFC. The expert-vs-lay gap suggests familiar readers are stricter than you or strangers.
- A **3-way variant**, as in the stylistic-fidelity setup above, shows a real reference excerpt and asks which of two candidates (model A vs model B, or real vs generated) better matches it. This mirrors the pairwise automated evaluators, so human and automatic agreement can be computed directly.
- Combine automated and human signals into a small dashboard per model version:
  - style similarity: StyleDistance plus LUAR, against your centroid and an impostor baseline;
  - AV accept rate;
  - classical feature z-score diff;
  - slop rate relative to your baseline;
  - detector score calibrated on your own text;
  - content or task score (MIS or SBERT);
  - periodic blind 2AFC "fooled" rate as the ground-truth check.
- Use the blind-test results to choose or weight the automatic metrics. This is the "validate the evaluator on your own data" step that Baumler et al. and Jangra et al. both imply.

### Gaps
- No study was found on authors' ability to recognize their own writing against an LLM imitation in a blind setting, and no accuracy numbers exist for that.
- The full title and author list of arXiv 2601.18353 were not confirmed from the fetched page. A search summary attributed it to Chakrabarty & Dhillon (CHI 2026), which is unverified.
- No guidance was found on the minimum number of blind trials used in published personal-style studies.
