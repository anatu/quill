# Housel alone first; neighbours must earn entry

Train the first adapter on Housel alone. Treat every similar author as a gated experiment that must beat that run on held-out Housel text. Two findings force this. First, the similarity pass found no neighbour anywhere near Housel's own ceiling. His two random halves cannot be told apart (StyleDistance AUC **0.50**, Burrows' Delta **0.75**). Every candidate can (AUC **0.87–1.00**, Delta **0.95–1.37**). Measured from a distant control towards that ceiling, no candidate closes more than **43%** of the gap on Delta or **24%** on AUC. Second, the literature shows that pooling authors helps only when the target has about 16 texts. No study tests it at Housel's 338k words, and the closest evidence says mixing *conflicts* when target data is plentiful. Added authors therefore bring a different voice, and no study predicts a fidelity gain from them. If the user still wants the test, the least-distant usable neighbours are **Ted Lamade, Shane Parrish's Farnam Street essays and Naval Ravikant**. The test design is a two-stage run: a tagged pool with Housel at about **74%** or more of target tokens and **≤100k** neighbour words, then a pure-Housel anneal. That holds neighbours to about **16%** of all training tokens. The way to add volume without dilution is more Housel. The Motley Fool holds an estimated **1.5–2.5M words** of his columns, but its robots.txt blocks ClaudeBot and its terms ban harvesting, so that route needs a written licence. The 342 cleaned posts already sit in the plan's T3 tier and are internally consistent. The real gap is coverage of registers Housel never wrote, and the neighbours do not fill it either.

Labels used below: **[M]** measured in the 2026-11-08 similarity pass, or counted from its raw files. **[L]** a published claim. **[I]** my interpretation of measured or published results. **[P]** my proposal. No style-specific evidence backs any [P] number.

## Every neighbour is separable from Housel; his own halves are not

**[M]** The pass scored 20 candidate authors against one random half of Housel's cleaned corpus (H-B: 171 posts, 182k words). The other half (H-A: 171 posts, 154k words) was scored like a candidate, which sets the ceiling. Two metrics carry the ranking. StyleDistance verification AUC trains a logistic regression on 20 passages per class, with cross-validation grouped by post; 0.50 means indistinguishable and 1.00 means perfectly separable. Burrows' Delta compares profiles of the 150 most frequent words on 4,000-word samples; lower is closer. Patrick McKenzie served as a distant human control. Two sets of Claude essays served as LLM controls: one imitating Housel from memory, one in default prose ([results.csv](/Users/natuanand/.quill/research/2026-11-08/simpass/results.csv); [measurement notes](<research_notes/Housel similar author corpora/similarity_measurements.md>)).

**[M]** Every candidate is linearly separable from Housel. Even Naval, the closest on the embedding metric, sits nearly three combined standard deviations above the ceiling. Lamade, the same-venue pick, scores **0.989** at 60 passages per class. That is level with McKenzie's 0.997 at the same size. The Tier 1 bar used in the pass (AUC ≤0.98 and Delta <1.10, i.e. "closer than McKenzie on both") admits eight authors. But it only says they beat a distant control. It does not say they are near Housel. **[I]** The mixture notes proposed a stricter gate: admit an author only if they close at least half the gap on two of three metrics. Nobody passes, even against McKenzie, a floor in another genre. A same-register impostor floor would be harsher still.

**[M]** The ranks are soft. AUC and Delta agree only weakly (Spearman ρ = 0.33 across the 20 candidates), and the simulated rank bands are wide. Treat the table as tiers, not an order. Brown's AUC used only 14 passages; an add-author test with 5 passages gave 0.83, so small samples bias AUC towards 0.5. Farnam Street kept only 15 posts, because early FS posts are mostly book excerpts. **[I]** Topic leaks into both metrics. StyleDistance is the most content-independent encoder tested, but it scores only 0.29 on STEL-or-Content ([StyleDistance](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.12757)). Finance writers who share Housel's topics (Lamade, Zweig, Brown) are probably flattered by that leak. Their true style gap is likely no smaller than measured. **[M]** The ceiling came from random halves, not topic-disjoint ones. **[I]** A topic-disjoint ceiling will sit somewhat above 0.50, so recompute it before using it as a gate.

**[M]** The pass also answers the user's worry about representativeness. The two random halves agree on every stylometric feature. Across the corpus, sentences average **14.7 words**, paragraphs average **32 words**, **44%** of paragraphs are one sentence, and "you" runs at **24.3 per 1k words**. Em dashes run at **5.7 per 1k** and colons at **6.1**. These traits are stable, not artefacts of a few posts. **[L]** STYLL-style per-author inverse paraphrasers need more than 100,000 words of the target ([Patel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08986)). StyleTunedLM's style curve flattens between 70% and 100% of a per-author set that a summarizer put at about 80k tokens (cosine 0.92 → 0.95), a reference size that still needs checking in the PDF ([StyleTunedLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.04574v1)). **[I]** Housel's corpus (342 posts, 337,898 words, about 440k tokens) clears the first threshold three times over and sits in the plan's T3 tier. That is enough to learn the voice. What it lacks is coverage of registers he never wrote, such as memos and technical explainers. Every Tier 1 neighbour is also an essayist on the same subjects, so neighbours do not close that gap.

## Published evidence favours pooling only for tiny targets

**[L]** Every published win for other-author data that the notes found has a target with about 16 texts. Patel et al. define low-resource as "just 16 posts (on average ~500 words)" ([Patel et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08986)). TinyStyler pools 88M Reddit comments and beats GPT-4 at that budget, with no ablation of target-author fine-tuning ([TinyStyler](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.15586)). AuthorMix, the closest 2026 work, mixes 2–4 similar-author LoRAs for a 16-text target. It peaks at k = 4 and degrades at k ≥ 7, and it warns that irrelevant adapters "can degrade the merged model" ([AuthorMix](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23069)). AuthorMix tests literary Gutenberg authors only. It flags "highly distinctive or contemporary styles" as untested.

**[L]** The data-rich evidence points the other way. In ASTRAPOP's preliminary experiment on a target with 2,000 training segments, "one model per author works better" than the pooled exemplar-based model ([ASTRAPOP](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.08043v2)). Dong et al. find that data composition "appears to enhance various abilities under limited data conditions, yet can lead to performance conflicts when data is plentiful". They add that the *amount* of composition data matters more than the ratio ([Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05492)). That is a general SFT result, not a style result. No study adds similar-author data to a target with 100k–500k words and measures fidelity to that target. Metric validity is also weak: AuthorMix's embedding-based style score did not track human judgments (ρ = 0.10), so automated metrics alone cannot certify a gain.

**[L]** The design analogues come from other tasks, not style. A final target-only phase helps: upsampling domain data in the last 10–20% of pretraining gave up to +8.26 pp on GSM8K ([Blakeney et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.03476)), and Dong's two-stage DMT keeps ability with replay as small as 1/256. Tags let a shared model separate sources. Metadata conditioning matched standard pretraining with 33% less data ([MeCo](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01956)), and fixed user identifiers beat prefix-tuning by up to 13% ([UserIdentifier](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00135)). Concatenating separate LoRAs beat data mixing by about 12% on one skill pair, but data mixing stayed best "for many skills" ([LoRA Soups](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13025)). Repetition is cheap up to about 4 epochs ([Muennighoff et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.16264)).

**[I]** Put the two findings together. The neighbours are distinct voices, and nothing published predicts a gain at this data size. On held-out Housel fidelity, the likely effect of augmentation runs from zero to negative. The plausible upside is narrow: reinforcing shared moves (anecdote, then principle; short paragraphs) and perhaps some regularisation against memorising Housel's text. The risk is concrete. Every recommended neighbour under-uses Housel's signature punctuation and his second person. A word-weighted pool of Lamade, Farnam Street and Naval runs at about **2.6 em dashes, 3.4 colons and 16 "you" per 1k words**, against Housel's **5.7, 6.1 and 24.3** (my calculation from measured rates). If the model ignores tags, a 26% neighbour share in stage 1 pulls the blended target towards 4.9 em dashes and 5.4 colons per 1k. The honest recommendation is plain: **Housel-only first, and augmentation only as a gated experiment.**

## The test mixture: three neighbours, capped, then a Housel-only anneal

**[P]** Everything in this section is a proposal. The ratios follow the mixture notes' D2 design and the user's "a little, not far" limit. None has style-specific evidence. Write shares in target tokens, and report absolute neighbour word counts beside them, because the amount matters more than the ratio ([Dong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.05492)). The arithmetic assumes about 280k Housel training words once ~10% is held out for test and ~7% for dev, at the plan's 1.3 tokens per word.

| Element | Stage 1: tagged pool | Stage 2: anneal | Whole run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Housel rows | Every train target once, with input form A (neutral paraphrase) | Every train target once, with input form B (generic draft) | **2× upsampling**: two passes, one per input form. Within the plan's T3 limit of 1–2 repetitions, and well under ~4 epochs |
| Neighbour rows | ≤100k words: **Lamade ≤40k, FS/Parrish ≤30k, Naval ≤30k** (k = 3) | Replay ≤2% of stage-2 tokens, drawn pro rata from stage-1 rows | ≤~106k words |
| Housel share of target tokens | **≥73.7%** | **≥98%** | **≥84%** (neighbours ≤~16%) |
| Per-author cap | ≤40k words, ≈10% of stage-1 tokens | — | ≤6% each |
| Tags (system line, masked from loss) | `voice: v0` for Housel; `v1`, `v2`, `v3` for neighbours | Same | Always `v0` at inference |
| Learning rate | 1e-4 cosine, warmup 10 updates | 5e-5 cosine, warmup 10 updates | Pick the LR on D0 first, then reuse it |

**[P]** A short neighbour supply is fine; do not top it up past the caps from other authors. Use opaque voice codes, not names. The project already keeps author names out of adapter names, file names and outputs. Chunk neighbour text to Housel's length distribution (150–800-word targets on paragraph boundaries). Hold out about 20% of each neighbour's passages for the evaluation impostor pool. Drop any neighbour passage that is a topical near-duplicate of a Housel test essay. Build neighbour pairs with the same inversion pipeline as Housel's, and keep only pairs where the target sits closer to Housel than its input does (the mixture notes' "style gap" filter). Score post-2022 neighbour posts with the slop detector, and drop those above Housel's 90th percentile.

**[L]** mlx-lm v0.32.2 (released 2026-10-16) has no per-sample loss weights, no sampling probabilities and no adapter merging ([releases](https://api.github.com/repos/ml-explore/mlx-lm/releases); [LORA.md](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/LORA.md)). Reading the source shows three behaviours that matter. A list of `hf_dataset` entries concatenates without weights. Batches are sorted by length. Loss is averaged over the tokens in a batch, so longer targets carry more gradient ([datasets.py](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/tuner/datasets.py); [trainer.py](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/tuner/trainer.py)). **[P]** The mixture therefore lives in the files. Duplicate rows to upsample, with a fixed-seed shuffle before writing. Put the tag in the system message, and let `--mask-prompt` keep it out of the loss. Fill each `valid.jsonl` with Housel dev items only.

```text
~/.quill/data/mlx/mix-a000-s1/{train,valid}.jsonl   # D0 stage 1: Housel form A only
~/.quill/data/mlx/mix-a000-s2/{train,valid}.jsonl   # D0 stage 2: Housel form B only
~/.quill/data/mlx/mix-a040-s1/...                    # + Lamade ≤40k (v1)
~/.quill/data/mlx/mix-a100-s1/...                    # + Lamade, FS, Naval ≤100k (v1–v3)
~/.quill/data/mlx/mix-a100u-s1/...                   # same rows, voice line removed
~/.quill/data/mlx/mix-aNNN-s2/...                    # Housel form B + ≤2% replay rows
~/.quill/data/mlx/aux-only/...                       # neighbour rows only (for D3)

{"messages":[{"role":"system","content":"quill v1\nop: restyle\ninput: paraphrase\nvoice: v0"},
             {"role":"user","content":"<neutral paraphrase>"},
             {"role":"assistant","content":"<Housel original>"}]}

# Stage 1
uv run mlx_lm.lora -c configs/train/sft.yaml --data ~/.quill/data/mlx/mix-a100-s1 \
  --adapter-path ~/.quill/adapters/mix-a100-s1 --iters <rows in train.jsonl>
# Stage 2: same rank, scale and num_layers; own YAML with lr_schedule arguments [5.0e-5, <decay>, 1.0e-6]
uv run mlx_lm.lora -c configs/train/sft-anneal.yaml --data ~/.quill/data/mlx/mix-a100-s2 \
  --resume-adapter-file ~/.quill/adapters/mix-a100-s1/adapters.safetensors \
  --adapter-path ~/.quill/adapters/mix-a100-s2 --iters <rows in train.jsonl>
```

**[P]** Three mechanics need a check before the grid runs. First, `--resume-adapter-file` loads adapter weights only, with no visible optimizer-state restore. Expect a fresh Adam state, which is why stage 2 has its own warmup. Second, the YAML `lr_schedule` sets the learning rate, so stage 2 needs its own config rather than a `--learning-rate` override. Third, the notes disagree on what `--iters` counts. The plan's config treats it as micro-batches, so at batch size 1, iters equal to the row count gives one pass. The mixture notes divide by gradient accumulation as well. Confirm on a 32-iteration probe by counting the examples consumed. A wrong reading would run 16 passes instead of one.

**[P]** For the cheap post-hoc dial (D3), train an `aux-only` adapter with the same rank and layer set as D0. Then merge the two by rank concatenation. Concatenate the A matrices along the rank axis and the weighted B matrices along the rank axis, and write an `adapter_config.json` with rank 32. mlx-lm's `LoRALinear` computes `y + scale·((x @ A) @ B)`, so with equal scales this gives an exact weighted sum of updates ([tuner/lora.py](https://github.com/ml-explore/mlx-lm/blob/main/mlx_lm/tuner/lora.py)). Sweep the neighbour weight w ∈ {0.1, 0.25}, plus a variant that applies it to middle layers only. Never average A and B separately; that adds cross-terms ([LoRA Soups](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.13025)). Mixing changes data volume, not peak memory. The only M4 Pro 24 GB anchor is a self-reported 6–10 GB for Qwen3-8B 4-bit SFT at 4k context ([mlx-lm-lora](https://github.com/Goekdeniz-Guelmez/mlx-lm-lora)). At the plan's assumed 150 tokens/s, one Housel pass of 280k words takes about 1.6 h, and 100k neighbour words add about 0.6 h.

## Eight runs and one rule decide whether augmentation stays

**[P]** Freeze the test set before any neighbour text is pulled. Embed every Housel post with a content encoder and cluster the posts (k ≈ 15). Hold out whole clusters totalling about 10% of words for test (≈34k words; roughly 60–100 items after chunking) and about 7% for dev. Keep reposts with their twins, and record the split hash. Recompute the within-Housel ceiling on topic-disjoint halves. Pick checkpoints on dev. Touch test once per finalist. Keep the plan's 30–50 real PM briefs as a coverage probe, since no reference text exists for them.

**[P]** Separate the instruments that selected the authors from the ones that judge the result. The neighbours were admitted on StyleDistance and Delta, and an author chosen for StyleDistance closeness will look less diluted on StyleDistance. For this decision, demote the plan's M2 (StyleDistance) and M6 (Delta) to diagnostics. Decide on a composite: the geometric mean of the M1 char-n-gram verifier's accept rate, the Housel share in a 5-way attribution classifier, and meaning preservation (MIS), with the entity gate applied first. Train M1 against impostors that include the held-out neighbour passages. The attribution classes are {Housel, Lamade, FS, Naval, LLM}, with the LLM class built from the Claude controls and B0 outputs. The neighbour share it reports is the direct dilution meter.

| Run | Design | Neighbour words | Question it answers | Est. training time |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E0a, E0b | D0: same two-stage schedule, no neighbour rows, two seeds | 0 | The control, and the seed-to-seed noise floor | 2 × 3.1 h |
| E1 | D2, tagged | 40k (Lamade) | Does the single nearest same-venue author help? | 3.3 h |
| E2 | D2, tagged | ≤100k (Lamade, FS, Naval) | Does a k = 3 pool help? | 3.7 h |
| E3 | E2 without the voice line | ≤100k | Do tags protect Housel's identity? | 3.7 h |
| E4 | D3: E0a adapter + aux-only adapter, w ∈ {0.1, 0.25}, plus middle layers only | ≤100k | Does a post-hoc dial beat pooling? | 0.6 h + minutes per merge |
| E5 | E0 and E2 on 30% of the Housel train set | 0 / ≤100k | Does the regime claim hold: help only when Housel data is scarce? | 0.9 h + 1.5 h |

The grid needs about 20 hours of training at the plan's assumed throughput: two to three nights, plus evaluation. Do not run it on the plan's 30% subsample for T3 ablations, because a smaller Housel set biases the result towards augmentation helping. E5 uses that bias on purpose, as a test. Stop early if E1 and E2 both fail the dilution checks on dev.

| Check, on the frozen topic-disjoint Housel test set | Pass condition **[P]** |
|---|---|
| Hard gates | Entity-gate pass rate no worse than E0; zero verbatim spans of ≥8–10 words against the whole Housel corpus |
| Verifier (M1, neighbours as impostors) | Accept rate ≥ E0 − 0.02 |
| Attribution | Housel share ≥ E0 − 0.03 and neighbour share ≤ E0 + 0.03 |
| Tic profile (em dash, colon, "you", sentence length) | Divergence from held-out Housel ≤ E0 |
| Gain | Composite above E0, with a paired-bootstrap 95% CI that excludes 0 and exceeds the E0a–E0b gap. Or the same test on the coverage-probe composite, with every dilution check still passing |
| Humans | Preferred over E0 in at least half of ≥30 blind matched pairs |
| Choice | The smallest neighbour amount that passes every row. If none passes, ship E0 and stop pulling neighbour text |

**[I]** Read E5 against E2. If neighbours help at 30% of Housel but not at 100%, that confirms the regime claim, and the case for augmentation at full size is closed. If E3 matches E2, the model is ignoring the tags. Confirm directly by generating dev outputs under `voice: v1`: if the attribution classifier does not shift, the tags carry no information.

## Claude's Housel imitation fails the embedding test and passes Delta

**[M]** Claude writing as Housel from memory is perfectly separable from Housel (AUC **1.000**). Its centroid cosine is the lowest of any row (**0.866**), below even Claude's default prose (0.879). Yet its Delta of **1.044** beats 18 of the 20 human candidates; only Farnam Street and Lamade score lower. The imitation is a caricature. It writes 9.6-word sentences against Housel's 14.7, 23-word paragraphs against his 32, and **no em dashes, semicolons or parentheses** against his 5.7, 0.9 and 0.9 per 1k. It overshoots "you" (32.4 vs 24.3 per 1k). Buffett and Wiggins sit nearer Claude's default prose than Housel on both metrics. No Tier 1 neighbour does.

**[I]** Four consequences follow. First, the library's worry that a Housel adapter "buys less over the prompt-only baseline" because LLMs imitate his cadence is not supported by this probe. On embedding and verifier metrics, the adapter has headroom, which backs the project's no-prompt-only rule. Second, function-word Delta cannot certify imitation. A ship test built on Delta would pass a prompt-only caricature, so M6 must stay diagnostic. Third, the probe is one model and one prompt, written from memory, with no style guide or exemplars. The plan's B0 has a frozen style guide, 5 exemplars and the same rerank, and it must be scored as specified, not assumed weak. Fourth, the imitation's errors give the adapter a concrete checklist: restore Housel's sentence length and his em-dash, colon and parenthesis rates. A neighbour pool that under-uses em dashes and colons pushes the same way the imitation fails. That is one more reason the tic-profile check sits in the decision rule.

## Conclusion

The similarity pass changes the question. The user's worry was volume, but the corpus already clears every published size threshold and holds steady across random halves. Housel's voice also proves distinctive enough that no human neighbour, and no prompted imitation, is mistaken for it. That distinctiveness is the asset the adapter exists to capture. It is also why mixing in neighbours costs more than it looks: each one is a separate voice with its own punctuation and pronoun habits, and a linear probe already tells them apart. The useful roles for the neighbours are as impostors that sharpen the verifier and, at most, as a capped and annealed regulariser that must prove itself on held-out Housel text.

The highest-leverage action costs no compute: send the Motley Fool a licence request this week, while D0 trains. A yes would roughly double Housel's own corpus without diluting it, and the existing pass can confirm the older columns' voice before a single row enters training. A no leaves D0 as the answer. Run E1–E5 only if the user wants proof, and expect them to confirm that the honest dataset is Housel alone.
