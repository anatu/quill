# Writing harnesses fix structure, not voice

Almost no literature treats the harness around a writing model as its object of study, and what exists says the harness and fine-tuning buy different things. The researchers found no paper that uses "harness" or "scaffold" with prose quality as the outcome, no fixed-model harness ablation for writing with human raters on a current model, no peer-reviewed prompt-optimiser result on writing, and no staged system that writes textbook exposition and is graded by teachers on the prose. The relevant evidence is scattered across plan-then-write generation (a few hundred papers since 2018), agentic report and survey writers (about forty systems, mostly 2024 and 2025), critique, verification and selection loops (a large literature, almost all on short tasks), and style personalisation. Across those, harness stages have produced human-judged gains of roughly **10 to 28 percentage points on organisation, coherence and relevance**, have **halved or better the contradicted, omitted and invented facts when each plan unit names the facts it covers**, and have added **5 to 15 points of factual precision** from one verify-and-repair pass. They have produced no measured gain on logical errors, and prose-quality scalars are flat or slightly worse under a harness. Author-level voice has been delivered only by fine-tuning: expert readers' odds of preferring AI text went from **0.16 with in-context prompting to 8.16 after fine-tuning** in the one preregistered study. The design under consideration (Claude plans typed moves with bound details, a LoRA-tuned Qwen3-8B realises them, Claude checks per plan item, the small model regenerates failures) therefore gives each model the job the evidence says it can do, and three of its parts are untested anywhere: typed explanation moves as a control signal, a per-item check-and-regenerate loop on expository prose, and repair by regeneration from the style model with both error counts and register measured. The documented risks are a plan the realiser was not trained to follow, the wrong detail chosen inside a unit and repeated across samples, and a checker that misses errors more often than it invents them.

## How to read the evidence labels

Every figure below carries three labels where they matter. Publication status is **peer-reviewed** (venue confirmed by the researchers), **preprint** (including papers whose venue was not confirmed this session), or **vendor/blog**. The judge is **human**, **LLM judge**, or **automatic metric**. Provenance is **verified** (the researchers read the number in the paper's text, through an automated page reader, so table values deserve a spot check before print), **recalled** (cited from the researcher's memory, not re-opened), or **snippet** (seen only in a search summary or a secondary review). A figure labelled recalled or snippet is a lead, not a result. Effect sizes from different papers use different metrics and are not comparable with each other.

## No field studies the writing harness by name

The census question has a direct answer: **the writing harness is not a recognised research area**. Four communities hold pieces of it and none shares a name for it. NLP generation holds pipelines measured on organisation and coverage. Personalisation and retrieval hold the adapter-versus-retrieval comparison, measured on ROUGE. Human-computer interaction holds co-writing interfaces; its design-space paper reviewed 115 papers and maps assistants by task, user and interaction, without measuring pipeline effects on output quality (peer-reviewed, CHI 2024) ([design-space paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.14117)). Agent evaluation holds the fixed-model harness studies, and those are about coding and task agents. The main harness position paper names no writing task ([arXiv 2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950)).

The trend has three phases. From 2018 to 2022 the work was trained planners for data-to-text, summarisation and stories, with matched baselines and human studies of 20 to 50 items. In 2024 it became genre-specific expository pipelines (wiki article, survey, research paper) scored by LLM-judged rubrics with a small human study attached. In 2025 and 2026 it became general deep-research agents and benchmarks, where the question shifted from "does a pipeline beat direct prompting" to "which product ranks higher on an LLM-judged benchmark", which makes harness-versus-model attribution harder to read from recent work. The evidence quality fell as the volume rose.

Four absences matter most for this project. The researchers' estimate is that papers holding the model fixed, varying the structure around it for writing and using human evaluation number in the **low tens for 2022 to 2026**, concentrated in grounded article generation and fiction; that estimate comes from about forty queries and is not a count. Targeted searches for prompt-optimiser results on long-form writing, for end-to-end optimisation of a writing pipeline against human preference, and for fixed-model writing-scaffold ablations each returned nothing; GEPA's abstract (peer-reviewed, ICLR 2026) names no writing task ([GEPA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19457)), and the only writing figure is a paywalled vendor claim of a "44 percent quality improvement" with no visible metric, judge or dataset ([Every](https://every.to/also-true-for-humans/this-prompt-optimizer-learns-from-its-mistakes-like-dna)). No system in the agentic census measures how its prose compares with human writing in style. And for textbooks specifically, the nearest work transforms an existing textbook (Google's Learn Your Way, preprint, with a 60-student randomised trial that did not isolate which component helped) or produces slides and scripts (Instructional Agents) ([Learn Your Way](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.13348); [Instructional Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19611)). These are absences in a targeted sweep, so each is "not found", not "proven not to exist".

Three patterns run through that table. The stage that carries the gain is early: retrieval, question-asking and the outline, not the final critique. AutoSurvey's refinement stage moved its content score from 4.57 to 4.56 while a writer-model swap moved it from 4.58 to 4.70 (preprint, LLM judge) ([AutoSurvey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10252)); SurveyX's rewriting stage mattered for citation F1 (81.52 to 55.16 when removed) and barely for content (4.59 to 4.55) (preprint, LLM judge) ([SurveyX](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14776)). The exception is TTD-DR, where sample-judge-critique-revise applied to every intermediate component was the largest contributor, on a weakly validated judge.

Second, **prose quality does not improve under a harness wherever anyone measured it**. Fluency was flat or slightly lower in every plan-then-write study that reported it: on par in Moryossef et al., 4.59 to 4.46 in ProGen ([ProGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15720)). The single-pass baseline has the best Language score in LLMxMapReduce-V2's table, 96.50 against 94.34 for the full system (preprint, LLM judge) ([LLMxMapReduce-V2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.05732)). AgentWrite raised breadth and depth about five points and lowered coherence and clarity about two (preprint, GPT-4o judge) ([LongWriter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07055)). DEER scores Format and Style highest for bare reasoning models and lower for deep-research systems (preprint, LLM judge validated against two experts at Pearson 0.73) ([DEER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17776)). The LAMP result is the one positive human-judged prose gain, and it comes from editing generic LLM prose within the same register, with introduced errors unmeasured.

Third, the baselines are often weak or absent. STORM's human study compares against an outline-using retrieval baseline, not single-pass generation; in OmniThink's own table plain retrieval-augmented generation beats STORM and Co-STORM on every rubric score (peer-reviewed, LLM judge) ([OmniThink](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.09751)). Agents' Room stories were about twice as long as the baseline's according to a secondary review (snippet), which confounds the human preference ([review](https://www.themoonlight.io/review/agents-room-narrative-generation-through-multi-step-collaboration)). Self-Refine's constrained-generation gain was beaten by a single-pass prompt that stated the constraint explicitly, 81.8 against 67.0 coverage, and adding the loop to the better prompt lowered it to 75.1 (peer-reviewed, ICLR 2024, verified) ([Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798)). Any harness result measured against a careless prompt overstates the harness.

### What fine-tuning buys instead

Fine-tuning's measured gains sit on the dimensions where the harness has none. In a preregistered study with 28 MFA-trained readers, 516 general readers and 10,920 pairwise judgments, expert readers' odds of preferring AI over human writers on stylistic fidelity were **0.16 under in-context prompting and 8.16 after fine-tuning GPT-4o on each author's works**; on writing quality, 0.13 and 1.87. A detector flagged 97% of prompted outputs and 3% of fine-tuned ones (preprint, human experts, verified) ([Chakrabarty et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.13939)). The prompting arm was single-shot, not an optimised pipeline, and the fine-tuned base was a frontier model. For small local models the evidence is narrower: Panza's fine-tuned 7B to 8B email models reached MAUVE 0.93 to 0.96 against 0.004 to 0.009 for GPT-4o with the user's history in context, and human judges found 28% to 32% of their emails "suspicious" against 24% for genuine ones and 64% for the untuned model (preprint, small human study, verified) ([Panza](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10994v4)). The one small-model rewriter with authorship metrics is negative: LoRA adapters on Qwen2.5 from 0.5B to 7B left authorship probability at 0.51 to 0.53 while LLM judges rated the output more than 20% less "AI-like" (single-author preprint, one user) ([InMyStyle](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.29238)). Fine-tuning can also buy general writing scores: 7B to 8B models tuned on 12,000 critic-filtered examples scored 7.44 and 7.39 against GPT-4o's 6.81, on an LLM critic that also selected the training data (preprint) ([WritingBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05244v4)); a 9B model trained by reinforcement learning against a frontier judge matched a 27B sibling in blinded human evaluation of stories (peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2026) ([POLARIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04095)).

Retrieval, a harness device, wins where the score rewards content: on LaMP, retrieval improved over a non-personalised model by 14.92% on average, per-user adapters by 1.07%, both together by 15.98% (preprint) ([Salemi and Zamani](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09510)). Content-similar exemplar retrieval made style worse in the one study that tested it: top-5 authorship attribution for email fell from 69.33 to 36.00 (peer-reviewed, verified) ([Catch Me If You Can? Not Yet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14543)).

The division of credit is therefore consistent. Training is credited, with blind expert reads, for register and for removing AI-typical surface. Structure around the model is credited, mostly on 2022 to 2024 models, for organisation, coverage, fidelity to supplied facts and coherence over length. Nothing found credits either side for expository clarity or explanation quality. No study compares a fine-tuned small model, a prompted frontier model in a multi-pass pipeline, and the combination, with blind human reads. The project's own measurement (register matched, about two major errors per passage) is the pattern this literature predicts for a small tuned model: Panza's style metric survived 4-bit quantisation while its content metric dropped from 0.265 to 0.207 BLEU ([Panza](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10994v4)).

How large a harness effect to expect is an open transfer question. On coding tasks with a verifiable success criterion, a controlled grid found harness changes moving pass rates 8.5 to 13.0 points against 2.5 to 5.0 for model changes (preprint) ([arXiv 2605.23950](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.23950)), and a pre-registered GAIA study found scaffold shifts of up to 28 points, largest for the strongest model (preprint) ([arXiv 2606.08529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08529)). Those tasks have an oracle; prose does not. The design under consideration manufactures a partial oracle, the plan, which is the reason to think some of that effect transfers.

## Each component has support; the composition has none

### Planning: well supported for content, on older and smaller models

The classical pipeline of text planner, sentence planner and realiser was built for exactly this problem, and the proposed design is structurally that split with a strong model as planner and a tuned small model as realiser ([Gatt and Krahmer](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1703.09902)). The classical evidence is architectural, with no controlled effect sizes. The neural evidence is quantitative and consistent: against a matched no-plan baseline, an explicit plan raised human pairwise coherence and relevance by roughly 10 to 20 points and cut contradicted or omitted facts by half or more, in studies of 20 to 50 items with low rater agreement. In the LLM era the measured benefit persisted on GPT-4, GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet, but every one of those comparisons is automatic or LLM-judged ([STORM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.14207); [LongEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.19103); [WriteHERE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08275)). The case for planning is strongest where the writer is weak, which is this design's situation: a training-free planning scaffold took Qwen-2.5-14B from 0.29 to 0.79 task completion on LongGenBench (preprint, automatic metrics) ([CogWriter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12568)). The evidence against is the generation gap, covered below, and one training result: adding plan-augmented data to LongWriter's fine-tune scored 75.0 against 77.3 without it ([LongWriter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07055)).

### Binding facts to plan units: the best-supported component

The three strongest fidelity results all attach specific facts to specific plan units: Moryossef et al.'s sentence plans, Fierro et al.'s extractive question blueprints (fully supported sentences 46.91% to 58.28%, human, 50 instances, peer-reviewed) ([Fierro et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.03381)), and Balepur et al.'s per-sentence content plans. Topic-level outlines show no such gain: STORM's verifiability was 3.80 against 3.85 for the baseline, and its editors named "over-association of unrelated facts" as a weakness. A plan that only orders topics does little for correctness. FROST adds a second lesson: generating an entity plan did not raise faithfulness; **editing the plan to drop unsupported entities** did, taking entity precision from 0.357 to 0.746 (peer-reviewed, verified; venue recalled) ([FROST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.07606)). The plan is a place to check content before any prose exists.

Binding has a known residue, and it is the project's main error class. In the macro-planning work the remaining errors were a wrong pitcher attached to a play and a percentage copied from the wrong team; in Balepur et al. they came from a competing fact in the retrieved set; in STORM the most common citation error was improper inferential linking. **Mis-attribution inside a unit, where the realiser picks the wrong one of several nearby facts, is what binding does not fix.** No retrieved study measures it as its own category in multi-paragraph expository text. The implication is that smaller units with fewer facts each leave less room for the error, which the literature suggests and has not tested.

### Typed moves: plausible, thinly tested, unproven for explanation

One clean ablation exists. Hua and Wang added a sentence-type label (claim, premise, functional) to keyphrase plans; removing it lowered human ratings on arguments from 3.99 to 3.03 for grammaticality, 2.78 to 2.26 for correctness and 2.61 to 2.03 for content richness, with 30 samples and three raters (peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2019, verified) ([Hua and Wang](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.00734)). Around it are weaker signals: giving BART the intended PDTB relation "significantly improves the consistency" of its realisation (peer-reviewed, abstract only) ([Stevens-Guille et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2022.sigdial-1.48/)); removing task types from WriteHERE's plan cut its LLM-judged story score from 2.143 to 0.717 (preprint) ([WriteHERE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08275)); TeachCraft credits a step that types sources by pedagogical intent, reporting a 67.8% human win rate against eight baselines on 40 lessons (peer-reviewed, ACL 2026, abstract only) ([TeachCraft](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1328/)).

The evidence against concerns the representation and its source. Full rhetorical-structure trees did not help: RSTGen matched baselines on coherence with gold trees and was worse with predicted trees (peer-reviewed, automatic metrics) ([RSTGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.12590)), and a later paper notes RST-conditioned output tends to fall below plain BART on overlap metrics ([INLG 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.inlg-main.40.pdf)). Label quality at inference was the binding constraint, which a strong planner addresses and nobody has tested. Xu, Li and Choi chose flat sentence-level functional roles over RST for long-form answers because roles describe how information serves the question ([Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11048)). A flat or shallow sequence of typed moves with bound content is the supported form. No retrieved paper conditions generation on an explanation-move inventory (motivate, define, example, contrast) and measures coherence or faithfulness against an untyped plan.

### Per-item checking: the right regime, never measured as a loop

The self-correction literature turned sceptical in 2024, and its conclusion favours this design's shape. The TACL critical survey finds no reliable self-correction from a model's own feedback and names three conditions under which correction works: reliable external feedback, fine-tuning for it at scale, or a task decomposable so that verification is easier than generation ([Kamoi et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.01297)). A strong external checker asking one narrow question per plan item meets the first and third. The early datapoint closest to the design is inside Self-Refine: Vicuna-13B could not run the loop on its own output, but with Vicuna drafting and ChatGPT supplying feedback and refinement, maths accuracy rose from 24.18% to 40.5% (peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2023, verified; maths, not prose) ([Self-Refine](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17651)). In that result the strong model also did the refinement, which the proposed design deliberately withholds.

The parts for a plan-conformance check are published: plans as ordered question-answer pairs that can be checked against the text (peer-reviewed, TACL 2023) ([Narayan et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.00397)), a recall-direction QA metric (recalled) ([QuestEval](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.12693)), and small entailment checkers reported to match GPT-4 on grounded fact checks at far lower cost (recalled) ([MiniCheck](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10774)). The researchers found **no plan-adherence metric with reported human agreement and no measurement of how often regenerate-on-failure converges for prose**. The three error classes map unevenly onto the check. Truncation is caught by plan-item recall and should be the most reliable. A detail attached to the wrong claim shows up as an unsupported claim if the plan states the right pairing. A missing step is a recall failure, and recall is where checkers are weakest: annotators found omissions in 52% to 85% of long book summaries through free-text comments, not claim labels (peer-reviewed, COLM 2024, verified) ([FABLES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01261)). A missing step is only checkable if the plan lists the step.

### Selection among candidates: helps to about N = 16, capped by the checker and by shared errors

Sampling raises the chance that a good candidate exists; realised gains depend on the selector. Without a sound verifier, selection on MATH with Llama-3-8B moved from 40.50% to 41.41% between 100 and 10,000 samples while coverage rose to 98.44% (preprint, verified) ([Brown et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787)). With an LLM judge as selector, consensus beat top-score picking for every judge tested: on MT-Bench, minimum-Bayes-risk selection gained 0.28 against 0.13 for best-of-N with a 7B judge, and 0.41 against 0.06 with a 70B judge, plateauing near N = 20 (peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025, verified; the evaluation is itself LLM-judged) ([Wu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02902)). Candidate quality beat candidate diversity: aggregating samples of the single best model scored 65.7 against 59.1 for mixing models on AlpacaEval 2.0 (preprint, verified) ([Self-MoA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00674)). The nearest architecture, Speculative RAG, has a tuned 7B drafter produce several drafts and a larger untuned model score them, gaining up to 12.97 points over the larger model alone, on short-answer QA (peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025, verified) ([Speculative RAG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08223)).

A ceiling for this project can be computed, and it is the researcher's arithmetic, not a literature result. If errors were independent across samples at a mean of two per passage, a Poisson model puts the chance of an error-free sample near 13.5%, so at least one clean sample appears in about 44% of passages at N = 4, 69% at N = 8 and 90% at N = 16. That is an upper bound for two reasons the literature documents: errors recur across samples, and the checker must recognise the clean one. Whole-passage selection also throws away the good parts of rejected candidates. Integrative Decoding recombines at decode time using only the generator's own logits, with gains of 1.1 to 15.4 points on biography accuracy that are visible at four samples (peer-reviewed, ICLR 2025, verified) ([Cheng et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01556)); it suits a locally hosted model and keeps the register, and it has not been tested on binding errors.

### Repair by regeneration: supported for voice, unmeasured for convergence

The case for having the small model regenerate, and keeping the strong model's hands off the prose, is the best-evidenced design choice after binding. When LLMs revised 86 student essays, pronouns fell 31% to 56% and adjectives rose 40% to 87% **even under a "minimal edits" instruction**, and every essay moved in a common direction in embedding space; human revisions showed lexical divergence of 0.2 to 0.3 where the least invasive model averaged 0.476 (preprint, March 2026, verified; the paper has some internal inconsistencies between abstract and body) ([How LLMs Distort Our Written Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18161)). Rewriting chains lose content as well: BLEURT against the original fell from 0.945 to 0.537 over 100 rephrasings by one model and to 0.427 with three models alternating (peer-reviewed, ACL 2025, verified) ([Mohamed et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.20258)). On the content side, RARR's minimal edits preserved intent in 90% to 96% of passages while a regenerating corrector reached higher attribution and preserved intent in 6% to 40% ([RARR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08726)). "Preservation" in the repair literature means intent and edit distance; no verification paper measures register after repair.

The evidence against is about what regeneration will not do. One verify-and-repair pass on long-form text leaves much of the error in place: CoVe's best result still has about 29% of atomic facts unsupported, from 44%. Most of any revision gain arrives in the first round: Self-Refine's per-iteration curves flatten after one revision. RARR did not revise later sentences that depended on a corrected claim, a specific hazard for explanation, where sentences build on each other. Resampling from the same model redraws from the same error distribution. And the weak-drafter result cuts both ways: Vicuna could not act on feedback itself, so handing the small model a critique is not the same as handing it a better plan. Nothing found measures pass rate as a function of retries for prose.

## Six failure modes the design has to survive

**The plan sets the ceiling, and its errors propagate.** Oracle plans beat predicted plans wherever both were run: argument BLEU 16.30 against 13.19 in Hua and Wang, and ProGen's distance metric 30.16 against 39.94. The classical literature called this the generation gap: early decisions have unforeseen consequences downstream. For a strong-planner and small-realiser pair the form it takes is a mismatch between the plans the realiser was trained on and the plans the planner emits. The documented remedy is training on noised plans; without noise ProGen's distance worsened from 39.94 to 47.18 ([ProGen](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.15720)). Training plans extracted from reference text inherit extractor errors, about 80% relation recall in Puduppully et al. ([Puduppully et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.00582)).

**Plan items are silently dropped.** Storyline words appeared in the story 75% to 78% of the time in Plan-and-Write ([Yao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.05701)); only 58.5% of DOC's leaf outline items were faithfully realised ([DOC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.10077)); on unseen domains 66.7% of Moryossef et al.'s sentence plans were realised with all entities ([Moryossef et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.03396)). The counter-example shows it is trainable: macro-planning output adhered to its predicted plan above 98% on RotoWire ([Macro planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02723)). A per-item coverage check is the direct defence, and CogWriter's ablation found per-unit plan adjustment the most valuable of its stages (0.55 to 0.45 when removed).

**Units expanded independently lose coherence.** Skeleton-of-Thought "ignores the dependencies between points" and scored worse on coherence and immersion under a GPT-4 judge, failing on maths (preprint; venue recalled) ([Skeleton-of-Thought](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15337)). AgentWrite's serial variant beat its parallel one on coherence by about 6% ([LongWriter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07055)). Survey systems report repetition and inconsistency from parallel section drafting, and WebWeaver's section-wise writing with only the cited evidence in view is the positive version of the same lesson. The realiser needs the prior text or the neighbouring moves in view.

**Revision strips voice.** The distortion and broken-telephone results above are the evidence. The LAMP study adds that expert writers themselves agreed on where the problems were with span precision of only 0.57, and that giving the rewriter the experts' spans did not beat LLM-detected spans, so the rewrite, not the detection, was limiting. An instruction to edit minimally does not hold the register; span limits and edit-distance budgets have support by analogy from grammatical-correction and code-editing work seen only as snippets.

**Errors are correlated across samples.** Across 349 models, two models that are both wrong chose the same wrong answer 42.3% of the time against 12.7% expected by chance, more so for related and more accurate models, and LLM judges inflate the accuracy of models whose errors they share (peer-reviewed, ICML 2025, verified; multiple-choice data across models) ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.07962)). No study measures within-model error correlation across temperature samples for long-form prose. A systematic mis-binding caused by an ambiguous plan item will recur in every sample, and more samples will not fix it.

**LLM judges detect errors weakly and prefer familiar text.** Holistic agreement with humans is good and error detection is not: the best LLM rater reached 58.2 F1 on unfaithful claims against 95.5 on faithful ones with the whole book in context ([FABLES](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.01261)). A pass is weaker evidence than a fail. Judges rate their own output above others' and humans'; presentation order flipped GPT-4's pairwise preference 25% of the time (peer-reviewed, NeurIPS 2024, verified) ([Panickssery et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076)); across eight models judges scored lower-perplexity text higher than human evaluators did (workshop paper, snippet) ([Wataoka et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.21819)). A vendor post describes an evaluator that "identified real issues, then talked itself into approving the work" until calibrated with scored examples ([Anthropic Engineering](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/harness-design-long-running-apps)). No study reports Claude-family self-preference magnitudes or judge bias between base-model-register and assistant-register text, so the size of this effect for a Claude checker reading Qwen prose is unknown. LLM judges of long text agreed with humans at Spearman 0.32 in HelloBench ([HelloBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16191)), and in the Instructional Agents study could not separate conditions that human instructors separated by 1.4 points on a 5-point scale ([Instructional Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19611)).

## Which moves help which readers

Discourse theory gives the plan its shape. Moore and Paris argued in 1993 that an explanation plan must record the intended effect of each part on the reader as well as the rhetorical relations between parts, because a system that does not know why it said something cannot re-explain it ([Moore and Paris](https://aclanthology.org/J93-4004/)). A typed move is that intentional layer, and their case for it (repair) is the proposed design's case for per-item checking and regeneration. Meyer's five top-level expository structures (description, sequence, comparison, cause and effect, problem and solution) sit one level up, as the type of a section that constrains which moves appear; the figures for instruction based on them were seen through secondary summaries ([Shanahan](https://shanahanonliteracy.com/blog/does-text-structure-instruction-improve-reading-comprehension)).

Educational research supports five text-level devices with moderate meta-analytic effects, and every one depends on the reader.

| Device | Meta-analytic effect | Scope and caveat | Provenance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Worked examples | g = 0.48; 55 studies, 181 effect sizes | Mathematics only, mostly school algebra and geometry; correct examples beat incorrect or mixed; adding self-explanation prompts reduced the effect | Paper read ([Barbieri et al. 2023](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-023-09745-1.pdf)) |
| Self-explanation prompts | g = 0.55; 69 effect sizes | Mostly undergraduates; questions (0.56) beat imperatives (0.40) | Paper read ([Bisra et al. 2018](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2018-bisra.pdf)) |
| Signalling (previews, headings, pointer phrases) | Retention g = 0.53, transfer g = 0.33; 103 studies | Largely multimedia; larger for low-prior-knowledge learners | Secondary summary, not checked against the paper ([summary](https://inspire.acu.edu.au/articles/signalling)) |
| Refutation of a misconception | g = 0.41; 44 comparisons, n = 3,869 | State the misconception, negate it, explain why | Paper read ([Schroeder and Kucera 2022](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s10648-021-09656-z.pdf)) |
| Removing interesting but irrelevant detail | Learners given such detail score lower; overall effect size not confirmed | Sources conflict on study count; one summary says the harm concentrates in short lessons | Press release and blog ([WSU](https://news.wsu.edu/2020/03/19/seductive-details-inhibit-learning/)) |

The reader-dependence is the main design input. McNamara and colleagues wrote four versions of a text crossing local and global coherence: low-knowledge readers learned more from the fully coherent version, and high-knowledge readers did better on inference and problem-solving measures with the minimally coherent one ([McNamara et al. 1996](https://scilit.com/publications/301a1d52e5db2c321b87abb1d565f76f)). A 2025 meta-analysis of about 60 studies reports that techniques which help novices can hurt experts (secondary summary) ([expertise reversal](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/one-size-doesnt-fit-all-teaching-experts-and-novices/)). So restatement, bridging and signposting should fall as assumed knowledge rises, and the plan needs an explicit reader-knowledge setting. The worked-example moderator warns against stacking devices. The seductive-details finding gives an operational rule: every realised sentence should trace to a plan item, and colour that carries no plan content is a cost. Almost all of this evidence comes from short lessons, multimedia or mathematics; evidence for long-form prose textbooks is thin, and no randomised test of move planning in generated text was found.

For the inventory itself, no published source gives a validated set of 6 to 12 moves for textbook prose with frequencies in real textbooks. The anchors are adjacent. Six sentence-level roles covered over 90% of sentences in explanatory answers, with examples at 5% to 13% and background at 18% to 39%, and the authors note textbook-style answers may need roles outside their scheme ([Xu et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11048)). Expert explainers in dialogue spent about half their turns explaining and about 14% testing prior knowledge ([Wachsmuth and Alshomary](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.02508)). About 28% of sentences in one open-textbook sample contained a definition, in a sample that may have been selected for definition density ([DEFT](https://aclanthology.org/W19-4015/)). Agreement bounds what a checker can be asked: human Fleiss' kappa on explanation moves was 0.43, rose to 0.70 on answer roles once commonly confused labels were collapsed, and was 0.31 on the roles of machine-written answers. Coarse distinctions (core statement, example, background, organisation) are reliable; fine ones are not.

One structural finding supports the hypothesis behind typed moves without testing it. LLM essays are reported as flatter and more paratactic than human ones, leaning on elaboration (peer-reviewed, ACL 2026, snippet) ([Fomsgaard et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1521.pdf)), and human texts show more structural variability in discourse motifs (peer-reviewed, ACL 2024) ([Kim et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10586)). A plan that forces contrast, consequence, example and refutation moves is a remedy in principle. None of these studies is on textbook exposition.

## The nearest precedents stop short of this design

**Balepur, Huang and Chang's Imitate-Retrieve-Paraphrase** (peer-reviewed, EMNLP 2023) is the closest. A small model writes a one-sentence stylistic content plan, a retriever fetches facts for it, and a paraphraser words the facts in the plan's style, about 1.2 billion parameters in all. It beat a retrieval-augmented LLaMA-7B on ROUGE-1 (0.490 against 0.169) and was rated above ChatGPT by two raters on 30 documents (4.75 against 4.41 on one dataset). Its texts were 73 to 90 words and its planner was small ([Balepur et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.03276)). **Moore and Paris** is the architectural ancestor: typed, intention-bearing plan nodes that support repair. **Question-answer blueprints** supply a plan format that is checkable by construction. **WebWeaver** has outline sections cite evidence IDs and a writer that sees only the cited evidence, binding at section scale. **Speculative RAG** is the small-tuned-drafter and large-untuned-verifier architecture, for short answers. **Self-Refine's Vicuna and ChatGPT pairing** is the weak-writer and strong-critic datapoint, on maths. **RARR** is the minimal-edit repair precedent. **SAG** has a large model write neutral content and a small trained model apply style, the reverse ordering, described only at snippet level ([SAG](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2410.03137)). **TeachCraft** is the nearest educational system, planning by pedagogical intent and ordering objectives from foundational to advanced.

Measured against those, four things would be new. The first is the division of labour: no retrieved paper pairs a strong LLM planner with a small fine-tuned realiser for multi-paragraph textbook prose, and no study evaluates "one model supplies correctness, another supplies voice" with both error counts and expert style judgments. The second is an explanation-move inventory used as a control signal and tested against an untyped plan. The third is a per-plan-item check with regeneration measured as a loop: detection rates against human labels, pass rate against retries, error counts before and after. The fourth is methodological: a fixed-model harness ablation for expository writing with human error annotation, which the census shows does not exist. A system that also measured register fidelity of the harness output against the target textbook would fill the gap the agentic literature leaves entirely open.

The opposing position deserves its statement. The harness's share of quality appeared to shrink at the frontier in some accounts: a frontier model with search and reasoning outscored dedicated deep-research products in DEER, and the Anthropic post reports removing components with each model release. The alternative to an inference-time harness is to spend the critic at training time, as POLARIS did, and move correctness into the small model's weights. The design's answer is that its realiser is an 8B model at the edge of its ability, where the harness dominated in the low-cost comparisons found (Instructional Agents' model choice made no significant difference, Friedman p = 0.789, while the multi-agent structure moved instructor ratings from 2.33 to 3.01). Both routes remain open, and the experiment below tells them apart.

## Design recommendations and the experiment that tests them

### Recommendations

**Make binding the unit of design.** Each move should carry its own details and nothing else, with few facts per unit, because the documented residue is choosing the wrong fact among neighbours inside a unit. State every reasoning step as its own plan item so a missing step becomes a coverage failure the checker can see. Have Claude check the plan for unsupported or misplaced details before any prose is generated, following FROST's result that the plan edit, not the plan, bought the faithfulness.

**Train the realiser on the planner's format, with noise.** Derive plans from the textbook's own passages with the same planner prompt that will run at inference, fine-tune the adapter to realise those plans, and inject plan noise as ProGen did. A realiser trained on loose notes and handed typed plans is the train-and-test mismatch the literature warns about, and the 98% plan adherence in macro planning came from a realiser trained on plans.

**Realise serially with prior text in view.** Give the small model the passage so far and the neighbouring moves. Parallel per-move expansion is the documented route to incoherence.

**Keep the move inventory coarse and bound.** Six to ten moves whose distinctions fall along the high-agreement lines, each defined by the content it must carry so that realisation is checked by content coverage even where the label is debatable. Count move frequencies in the target textbook first; no published source supplies them. Add a reader-knowledge setting that scales restatement and signposting, and avoid stacking devices on one concept.

**Ask the checker narrow, plan-anchored questions.** "Does the passage state item k?" and "Which sentence carries detail d, and is it attached to claim c?" resemble claim checking, where agreement with careful humans is high. Do not ask which passage is better. Hide provenance and randomise order whenever candidates are compared. Derive the questions from the plan without the draft in view, following CoVe's finding that verification done independently of the draft outperformed verification that saw it (63.7 against 60.8 FActScore). Calibrate the checker against human labels on the project's own error taxonomy before trusting a pass; the literature gives no transferable miss rate.

**Select per item, with small N.** Use four to eight samples and choose by per-item pass counts or consensus, not a holistic score. Measure how often the same item fails across samples; if failures cluster on particular items, change the plan, not the sample count. Integrative Decoding over the small model's own logits is worth one trial as a register-preserving way to recombine samples.

**Repair by regeneration, bounded.** Regenerate the failed span or passage with the small model, cap at one or two rounds, and after any repair re-check the sentences that depend on the repaired one. When an item fails repeatedly, escalate to a plan edit by Claude (split the item, restate the binding), not a prose edit. If Claude must touch prose, restrict it to the flagged span under an edit-distance budget and re-measure register afterwards.

**Catch truncation outside the model.** A passage that stops early fails plan-item recall on its last items; a length and last-item check costs nothing.

**Compare against a strong single-pass prompt.** Before crediting the loop, run the best one-shot prompt to the small model with the bound plan stated clearly, since that baseline beat a check-and-revise loop in the one study that ran it. Ablate one component at a time.

### Minimal experiment plan

The experiment holds both models fixed and varies only the structure around them, on the same set of topics, with human error annotation as the primary outcome. About 40 passages per arm is in line with the literature's 20 to 50 items; under a Poisson assumption with a baseline of two errors per passage, a paired comparison of 40 passages has a standard error near 0.27 errors per passage, so a halving would be clearly visible and a 20% change would not. That power figure is this report's arithmetic.

| Arm | Planner input to realiser | Check and repair | What it isolates |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Loose notes (current setup) | None | Baseline, about two major errors per passage |
| B | Untyped plan, details bound per item | None | Value of planning and binding alone |
| C | Typed moves, details bound per item | None | Value of the type labels over B |
| D | Typed, bound plan corrected by a human | None | Plan-quality ceiling; what the realiser does with a perfect plan |
| E | As C | Claude per-item check; small model regenerates failures, up to two rounds, N = 4 | Value of the check-and-regenerate loop |
| F | As C | Claude per-item check; Claude rewrites failed spans | Voice cost and error benefit of strong-model repair |
| G | As C, realised by Claude directly | None | Content ceiling and register floor |

| Measurement | How | What it shows |
|---|---|---|
| Major errors per passage, by class (mis-bound detail, missing step, truncation, other) | Human annotation, blind to arm, two annotators on a subset for agreement | Primary outcome; no agreement figure for this unit exists in the literature, so the noise floor has to be measured here |
| Plan-item realisation rate | Per item, human-checked on a subset | Whether items are dropped silently |
| Checker precision and recall against human labels, per error class | Compare Claude's per-item verdicts with the annotation | Whether the checker is the bottleneck; recall on missing steps is the number to watch |
| Shared-failure rate across samples | Share of items that fail in at least three of four samples | Whether errors are systematic (plan or realiser) or random (fixable by resampling) |
| Pass rate by retry round | Log each regeneration | Convergence, unmeasured in the literature |
| Register after harness | The same instrument that established the register match, plus a blind expert read on a subset, arms E, F and G against the human textbook | Whether the loop or the repair strips voice |
| Coherence of repaired passages | Human rating of repaired against unrepaired passages | Whether corrections propagate to dependent sentences |

The readings are as follows. **The harness is the limiting factor** if B or C cuts errors substantially against A, D adds little over C, and E removes most of what remains with register intact: the small model can write correct prose when told exactly what to say, and the project's remaining work is plan and loop engineering. **The plan is the limit** if D is far better than C; effort goes into the planner prompt and plan checking. **The realiser is the limit** if errors persist under D and the shared-failure rate is high; the remedy is at training time, with plan-conditioned or noise-augmented fine-tuning, or a judge-in-the-loop route like POLARIS. **The checker is the limit** if humans find errors in passages E passed; no amount of sampling helps until detection improves. **Typed moves earn their place** only if C beats B on errors or on human coherence ratings; if not, the types are documentation. **Regeneration is justified over rewriting** if F's error rate is not much better than E's while its register score is worse; if F is much more accurate with register intact, the voice concern was overstated for this genre. If the two-per-passage baseline was itself produced by an LLM checker, validating that count against human labels is the first step, since error detection is the documented weak side of LLM judges.

## Conclusion

The prior art changes the question. "Can a harness substitute for training" has a negative answer for voice and a positive one for structure and fidelity, so the live question for this project is whether a plan can serve as the oracle that prose normally lacks. The coding-agent results show harness effects of 8 to 28 points where success is verifiable, and every writing result with a large fidelity gain is one where the plan made part of the output verifiable by binding facts to units. The design's value depends less on the cleverness of the loop than on how much of "correct" the plan can be made to state in advance, and its two hardest error classes sit exactly where a plan is silent: the choice among nearby facts within a unit, and the step nobody listed.

That also locates the contribution. Component by component the design repeats prior art, and the notes found no published measurement of the composition: no error counts on expository prose before and after a plan-anchored loop, no checker miss rates on explanation errors, no convergence curves for regeneration, no register measurement after repair, and no fixed-model writing-harness ablation with human raters. An experiment of seven arms on forty passages would produce each of those numbers for one genre and one model pair, and would be among the first of its kind.
