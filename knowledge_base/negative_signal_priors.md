# Labeled AI pools rebuild quill's slop yardsticks

Date: 2026-11-07. Question: beyond social media and Wikipedia, where can quill harvest large volumes of text that is AI-generated with high probability; how should those priors combine into calibrated labels; and what should change in the build plan? Target voice: Patrick McKenzie (`style_library.md`). Sources: the six notes in `research_notes/AI text weak supervision priors/`, the first negative-signal pass (`negative_signal.md`) and its notes, `plan.md` and `style_library.md`. Where the earlier pass and the new notes disagree, the new notes win.

Method labels on numbers: **measured** (a researcher ran the query on 2026-11-07), **computed** (counted from released files; **computed here** means counted for this report from the coordinator's 11 WildAI label shards or a Hugging Face API listing), **self-reported** (dataset card, operator or company claim), **reported** (a paper's own result), **vendor** (a detection company's measurement), **estimated** (extrapolation or inference in the notes), **derived** (arithmetic done here), **proposed** (a threshold set here with no published basis). Dev days and compute are planning estimates.

Three pools beyond Wikipedia and social media supply AI-written informational text at scale with measured precision. Pangram's WildAI corpus labels **32.5M web documents from 2024 to mid-2026 as AI**, and each carries topic and format tags, so it can be matched to human text from the same crawl ([WildAI card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/pangram/WildAI)). Claude Code stamps a default footer on GitHub pull requests: **22.4M bodies, 0.95-1.00 precision**, written mostly by Claude Opus 5.5 in October 2026 ([GitHub search](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=is:pr+in:body+%22Generated+with+Claude+Code%22+created:2026-10-01..2026-10-31)). Grokipedia offers **385k AI-written articles** title-matched to Wikipedia ([Triedman & Mantzarlis](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09685)). Time supplies the human side. Crawls before December 2022 are near-clean, while post-2023 dates carry only a 9-50% AI prior, so dates belong in anchors and class priors. The priors combine best through simple rules: one-sided anchors, WildAI's Pangram labels with Mixed set aside, per-source thresholds at a fixed false-positive rate, a gold-only baseline and leave-one-source-out tests. A Snorkel label model cannot estimate accuracies for priors that never fire on the same documents. For quill, the find rebuilds measurement and leaves training alone. Every new label certifies provenance, the pools over-sample easy unedited AI, and detectors trained on them miss author-fine-tuned output (Pangram caught 3% of it). The adapter's loss still takes no harvested text, and a general de-slop pre-stage fails the plan's fact-closure rule. The scorers change: a lexicon mined at scale against two human controls, a negative eval set of about 10k items in McKenzie's topics, a Claude-fingerprint residue metric and a corpus-level α metric. That costs **about 5.75 required dev days**, plus a 3-4 day local classifier if Fast-DetectGPT fails validation.

## 1. Verdict: the first pass undercounted AI text and made the right training call

- **The first pass was wrong on volume.** It found only Wikipedia's 7,648 tagged pages as a strong source. AI-provenance text in informational registers now exists by the tens of millions. WildAI holds 32,540,951 AI, 58,914,075 human and 4,587,420 Mixed documents (self-reported card counts, summed here), and about **13M of its AI documents sit in Tutorial, Nonfiction Writing, Knowledge Article and Listicle formats** (estimated, ±30% or worse) ([WildAI](https://huggingface.co/datasets/pangram/WildAI)). Claude Code PRs add 22,398,119 bodies (measured), and Grokipedia's 881,743 title maps (measured) pair AI articles with Wikipedia ([title map](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lewoniewski/grokipedia-wikipedia-16-languages)).
- **The first pass was right about what labels mean.** Every new pool certifies provenance through disclosure, construction or a detector. None certifies bad writing. Wikipedia tags and the expert sets (Human Detectors, Beemo, LAMP) remain the only human judgments of AI text, and they stay small.
- **The training recipe stays.** No harvested text enters SFT or any preference set. Topic and format matching gives a harvested negative no shared prompt with McKenzie's chosen text, and no study supports unpaired negatives for a style adapter. A general de-slop pre-stage fails fact closure: non-CC Grokipedia pages share only **14-38% of their named entities** with the 2021 Wikipedia article (measured, n=6, in a pilot against December 2021 revisions).
- **The measurement layer changes.** Lexicon v0 gains two families mined at scale against two human controls. The negative eval set grows from about 2.3k to about 10k items in quill's formats and McKenzie's topics. Two metrics arrive: Claude-fingerprint residue (M14) and corpus-level α (M13). McKenzie's own post-2022 essays get a changepoint check.
- **Claude Code PRs are the most quill-specific find.** They are current, self-labeled Claude prose. Of the 100 most recent October 2026 Claude co-author commits, **76 name Claude Opus 5.5** and 10 more its 1M-context variant (measured) ([commit search](https://api.github.com/search/commits?q=%22Co-Authored-By:+Claude%22+committer-date:2026-10-01..2026-10-31&sort=committer-date)). Their register is PR changelog prose (61% headings, 79% bullets, measured), so they cross-check Claude tells mined from content-matched Phase 3 drafts. They cannot stand in for those drafts.
- **Detectors stay diagnostic.** A classifier trained on WildAI is a distilled Pangram. It should beat Fast-DetectGPT as M10, and it inherits Pangram's blind spot: **3% detection on author-fine-tuned outputs** against 97% on prompted ones ([Chakrabarty et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13939v3)). It never becomes a reward and never certifies quill's outputs. Build it only if M10 fails validation.
- **Time labels humans well and AI badly.** A commercial detector flagged 2 of 20,100 pre-2022 pages ([Visokay et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.36631v1)). Post-2023 text is AI with probability 9% (US newspapers) to about 50% (vendor "new articles"). Use dates for anchors, class priors and same-URL pairs.
- **Combine with rules.** One-sided anchors never co-fire with each other, so a label model cannot identify their accuracies, and WildAI already supplies the strongest two-sided labeler. A gold-only baseline, per-source thresholds and leave-one-source-out tests are mandatory.

**WildAI is the pivot of this pass.** Russell, Glickenhaus, Thai, Wieting, Iyyer, Spero and Emi built it from FineWeb dumps CC-MAIN-2024-10 to 2025-26 and Common Crawl 2025-30 to 2026-25. EditLens preselected candidates, and Pangram labeled each document Human or AI only when three 512-token windows agreed ([WildAI card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/pangram/WildAI)). That design buys precision and costs representativeness. All 300,000 AI rows in the coordinator's sample came from the EditLens-AI preselection bucket (computed here), so the AI side is text that two detectors agree on: **the easy, unedited end of AI writing**. The human side from 2024-26 holds whatever Pangram missed. Pangram's misses are concentrated where quill lives: it flagged 3% of author-fine-tuned outputs, and it missed 10.10% of five-shot style imitations and 25% of scientific ones ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/ai-detectors-false-negatives)). The topic labels fit McKenzie unusually well. Finance & Business is the largest topic in the four informational formats, at roughly **2.0M AI and 1.9M human documents** (computed here from the 11 shards; ±30% or worse because shards are block-ordered). Personal Blog, the format nearest his essays, adds about 0.4M AI documents (estimated). The license is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, fine for private non-commercial work and a live question if quill's outputs go into paid work.

**Claude Code PRs are the freshest and most Claude-specific pool.** Monthly counts rose from 163,358 in January 2026 to 7,293,935 in October (measured), and footer PRs made up about 22% of all PRs created that month (estimated ratio). The footer is on by default and users can hide it ([Claude Code settings](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/settings-reference.md)). Disclosure can also vanish overnight: Codex's task-link marker fell from 426,398 PRs in October 2025 to 97 in December (measured) ([Codex query](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=is:pr+in:body+%22chatgpt.com/codex/tasks%22+created:2025-12-01..2025-12-31)). The register is a technical changelog. 61% of bodies use markdown headings, 79% use bullets, and about 6% are mostly non-Latin script (measured). The human control has to come from other repos: none of 20 sampled Claude-PR repos had a PR before 2023, and 17 were created in 2026 (measured) ([repo example](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=repo:we-promise/sure+is:pr+created:%3C2023-01-01)). GH Archive stopped carrying PR bodies after 2025-10-07, so bulk text needs search or GraphQL ([GitHub changelog](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-08-08-upcoming-changes-to-github-events-api-payloads/)).

**Grokipedia is two corpora under one name.** The 496,058 pages carrying the CC notice are light rewrites of Wikipedia (0.90 mean chunk similarity). The 385,139 without it are full AI write-ups (0.77, bimodal), with a median length about 4.6× the Wikipedia article ([Triedman & Mantzarlis](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.09685)). Its house style is unusual: Flesch-Kincaid grade 19.3 against Wikipedia's 12.8, and 21.4 references per 1k words against 90.2 ([Yasseri & Mohammadi](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.26899v2)). It is one model family in one pipeline, so it works as an out-of-distribution check on lexicons and classifiers. It is too narrow to anchor either one.

The remaining sources mostly set priors or fill gaps. Constructed 2025-model pair sets give generator diversity and humanized variants that wild pools lack. Journal declarations label AI-polished human text, a separate class from AI-written text. Producer signals rank candidates and never label them. He et al.'s site-level method is the best-measured producer lever, but WildAI's Tutorial slice (about 4.4M AI documents, estimated) already covers how-to text, so replication is optional.

### 3.2 Combine with rules; keep the label model optional

**Use simple rules first.** A document takes an anchor label (LF1, LF2, LF3) whenever one fires, after stripping the marker. Otherwise it takes WildAI's label (LF4), and Mixed documents go to a hard eval slice. Everything else abstains. LF5 to LF11 and LF14 audit the pools, rank unlabeled candidates and feed an optional label model. The reasons are specific. A generative label model learns accuracies from co-firing, and quill's certain priors split into disjoint regions: "pre-2022 means human" never votes on a document that "Claude Code footer means AI" covers. Their accuracies are unidentifiable, and every AI LF is conditioned on a post-2022 date, which breaks Snorkel's conditional independence ([Snorkel LabelModel docs](https://snorkel.readthedocs.io/en/v0.9.7/packages/_autosummary/labeling/snorkel.labeling.model.label_model.LabelModel.html)). FlyingSquid's triplet method needs three conditionally independent sources on the same items. WildAI changes the arithmetic further. It already labels ~96M documents with the detector that has the best independent nonfiction record, so a label model over weaker LFs would mostly learn to follow Pangram.

**A gold-only baseline is mandatory.** Without clean validation data, weak-supervision methods performed about as well as the raw weak labels or worse, and training directly on more than 10 clean examples per class beat them on most datasets ([Zhu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.17442)). Quill has about 9.5k gold-ish labels: Human Detectors (300), Wikipedia tags (~7.6k) and Beemo informational rows (~1.6k). A classifier trained only on those sets the bar. The weak pipeline ships only if it beats that bar on held-out sources.

**PU learning adds little here.** The one AI-text PU method (MPU) gained only about 0.4-2.6 F1 over strong supervised baselines, and sentence dropping produced most of its short-text gain ([MPU](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18149v4)). The positives also violate SCAR, the "labeled positives are a random sample" assumption most PU methods need ([Bekker & Davis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.04820)). Leaks, disclosures and WildAI's detector-agreed AI are the least-edited AI text. Patch the gap with variants: paraphrased, polished and humanized text from DetectRL-X, APT-Eval and HAP-E-2, plus WildAI Mixed as a hard eval class. Strip leak strings and markers before training. An nnPU term on the unlabeled pool, with π from α, stays out of v1.

**Keep adapter outputs out of the generic classifier.** The methods note suggests adding quill's adapter generations as positives. That would teach the classifier to flag every adapter output, which answers a question quill already knows the answer to: every output is AI. The plan's M1 authorship verifier already measures adapter-versus-author distance, and the blind tests measure what readers notice. The generic classifier should answer one narrower question: does this output resemble the generic AI pools? It can answer that only while it has never seen the adapter.

Scorer validation ranks first because a broken scorer corrupts every later decision. The first pass already showed that slop-score's lists are mostly fiction vocabulary. The new pools let each M7 family face 2,000 format-matched WildAI documents in McKenzie's topics before it gates anything.

The detector rows rest on hard evidence. Under a day of DPO against a detector's "human" score cut a RoBERTa detector's AUROC from 0.84 to 0.62, and the evasion transferred to other detectors ([Nicks et al.](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/1f9f07df0992ce21698d800eaa891bd8-Abstract-Conference.html)). Pangram scored near random on writing-quality pairs that did not hinge on AI versus human ([WQRM paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.07532v3)). Open supervised detectors collapse out of distribution: a ModernBERT-large detector with in-distribution AUROC above 0.99 fell to 0.856 on Beemo ([Vanguard card](https://huggingface.co/ShantanuT01/vanguard-ai-text-detector)). Pangram itself needed about 28M confirmed-human documents and hard-negative mining to bring FPR from 2.29% to 0.02% ([Pangram report](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.14873v3)). A WildAI-distilled classifier borrows that work through the labels, which is why it can beat Fast-DetectGPT. It cannot see what Pangram cannot see.

α deserves its caveat. Liang's estimator was 3-5× more accurate than instance-level detectors at estimating corpus fractions ([Liang et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07183v2)), and it resists per-sample gaming. It reads adjectives, though. After author fine-tuning, cliché density's share of the detector effect fell from 16.4% to an insignificant 1.3% ([Chakrabarty et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13939v3)). A near-zero α on adapter outputs therefore says the adjectives are fixed and says nothing about structure. HAP-E shows where structure hides: GPT-4o uses present participial clauses at 5.3× the human rate and nominalizations at 2.1× ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)). Both rates belong in M7's structure family and can be validated on WildAI.

### A general de-slop pre-stage fails fact closure

The idea is to train the adapter first on generic AI-to-human pairs (Grokipedia paragraphs, same-URL rewrites), then on McKenzie. The notes reject it on four counts. First, natural pairs do not share facts. Non-CC Grokipedia pages matched 14-38% of the 2021 article's entities and 0.2-1.1% of its 4-grams (measured, n=6), and heavily rewritten refresh pages were re-titled, re-scoped and a median 1.42× longer (measured). Plan Phase 3's first curation rule drops any pair whose target holds facts missing from its input, because such pairs teach invention. Paragraph-level alignment recovers some fact-matched pairs, at an unmeasured yield. Second, the human side is the wrong voice: Wikipedia and 2022 HubSpot prose are exactly the generic explainer register McKenzie was chosen to escape. Third, the plan already contains the right de-slop stage. Its SFT maps a generic Claude draft of each McKenzie passage back to the original, with facts closed by construction. Constructed pairs from instruction-tuned models reproduce the human-AI style gap robustly ([Reinhart et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.16107v2)), and the content-pairs note concludes that pairs built from the author's own pre-2022 corpus should be the backbone, with natural pairs as a small check (inference; no head-to-head study exists). Fourth, the one public general de-slop rewriter, Unslopper, was trained on about 1,000 Gutenberg pairs and its author warns of local coherence problems ([Unslopper card](https://huggingface.co/N8Programs/Unslopper-GGUF/blob/main/README.md)). No study shows that a generic stage improves later author-specific SFT. Verdict: skip it. If M14 shows the Claude fingerprint surviving restyle, the fix is more Claude-drafted pairs of McKenzie's own text.

### The Claude PR stream turns the fingerprint risk into a metric

The plan's risk table already lists "Residual Claude fingerprint" and watches it through the detector and M2. `style_library.md` makes `write` draft-then-restyle the default, so every runtime input is now a Claude draft, and the plan builds the measuring instrument as a side effect. Phase 2 and Phase 3 drafts come half from Claude and half from a local model, written from the same fact lists. Train a word and character n-gram probe to tell the two drafters apart on held-out raw drafts; an embedding classifier names five frontier families at 97.1% ([Sun et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12150)), so high AUC is plausible. Then run the probe on the adapter's restyled outputs, grouped by which drafter wrote the input. If outputs still reveal their drafter above chance, the fingerprint survived. Pair it with a residue ratio over the Claude family lexicon: R = (f_output − f_McKenzie) / (f_draft − f_McKenzie), where 0 means fully removed and 1 means untouched. The Claude PR stream keeps that lexicon current. A Claude tell enters the lexicon only if it shows up in Phase 3 Claude drafts against McKenzie (same content, deployment register) and in October 2026 Claude PRs against pre-2023 human PRs (current model, register-matched). Each quarter, pull a fresh month of PRs keyed by the model named in commit trailers.

### 5.3 What stays

SFT on style-inversion pairs with McKenzie's text as targets. The optional ORPO or CPO stage with the SFT model's own samples as rejected. Best-of-N with hard gates on entities, meaning, memorization, length and register. Separate rerank and eval models. M10 as a diagnostic. Blind tests as the final arbiter. No detector, crowd label or harvested text in any loss.

### 6.1 WildAI (informational slice and natural sample)

Format and topic strings match WebOrganizer's labels, such as "Tutorial", "Knowledge Article", "Nonfiction Writing", "Listicle", "Personal Blog" and "Finance & Business" (computed here from a label shard).

```python
            and not r["truncated"] and 300 <= r["token_count"] <= 3000)
ai = load_dataset("pangram/WildAI", "ai", split="train", streaming=True).filter(keep)
hu = load_dataset("pangram/WildAI", "human", split="train", streaming=True).filter(keep)
mx = load_dataset("pangram/WildAI", "mixed", split="train", streaming=True).filter(keep)
```

3. `source == "fineweb"` keeps the FineWeb v1.4.0 extraction, the same pipeline as the pre-2022 anchor, which removes an extraction confound. Add `format_score` and `topic_score` ≥ 0.6 and `pangram_fraction_ai` ≥ 0.9 (AI) or `pangram_fraction_human` ≥ 0.9 (Human) after inspecting their distributions (proposed thresholds).
4. Match each AI document to a Human document with the same `dump`, `format`, `topic` and token bucket. Cap 20 documents per registered domain (from `url`). Split train and test by domain.

### 6.3 GitHub: Claude Code PRs and pre-2023 human PRs

1. Endpoint: `GET https://api.github.com/search/issues` with an authenticated token, `per_page=100`, pages 1-10. Search returns at most 1,000 results per query, at roughly 30 requests a minute ([GitHub search docs](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/search/search#search-issues-and-pull-requests)).
2. Query: `is:pr in:body "Generated with Claude Code" created:2026-10-15T12:00:00Z..2026-10-15T12:04:59Z`. October 2026 averaged about 235k footer PRs a day (derived from 7,293,892 / 31), so a 5-minute slice holds about 820 and stays under the cap (derived). Spread slices across days and hours.
3. Keep bodies containing the literal footer `🤖 Generated with [Claude Code](https://claude.com/claude-code)`. Generic phrases are unusable: "Generated by Claude" matched 2.87M October PRs and only 1 of 100 contained it (measured) ([query](https://api.github.com/search/issues?q=is:pr+in:body+%22Generated+by+Claude%22+created:2026-10-01..2026-10-31)).
4. Strip the footer, `Co-Authored-By` lines, CodeRabbit blocks between `<!-- This is an auto-generated comment: release notes by coderabbit.ai -->` and `<!-- end of auto-generated comment: release notes by coderabbit.ai -->`, HTML comments, badges and URLs. Remove fenced code. Drop bodies with more than 20% of tokens in inline code, under 150 words after stripping, or under 90% Latin script. MinHash-collapse templates at Jaccard ≥0.85. Cap 20 per repo.
5. Optional model join: `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}/pulls/{number}/commits`, then parse `Co-Authored-By: Claude <model> <noreply@anthropic.com>`. Optional edit audit: GraphQL `userContentEdits` on 200 sampled PRs, which would measure how much humans edit the bodies.
6. Human control: `is:pr created:2022-06-01T00:00:00Z..2022-06-01T00:04:59Z` with the same paging. 2022 averaged about 207k PRs a day (derived from 75,617,364), so 5-minute slices also fit. Exclude bot accounts such as dependabot and renovate (pre-LLM templated text; inference), apply step 4's filters, and match the Claude sample's repo-language mix via `GET /repos/{owner}/{repo}`.
7. Yield: about 65% of October footer PRs pass the script and length filters (derived from 0.94 × 0.69); the code-density pass rate is unmeasured, so pilot 1,000 first. 40k kept bodies take roughly 30-40 minutes of search quota at 3,000 items a minute with 2-3× filter loss (derived). For volumes beyond search, GraphQL at 100 PR nodes per request reaches roughly 500k bodies an hour per token (estimated, untested).
8. Terms: PR text is user content under the GitHub Terms of Service, and repo licenses do not settle training use. Keep it local and get legal review before any redistribution.

## 7. Open questions and gaps

1. **WildAI's exact composition.** Per-format and per-topic totals come from 11 block-ordered shards; a pass over the 9.46 GB `labels` config gives exact counts. The paper (arXiv:2609.40295) was not read, and the card reports no precision for the three-window rule on this pool. The undetected-AI share of WildAI Human is unknown.
2. **License exposure.** WildAI is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 and Grokipedia's non-CC text is non-commercial. Whether a locally trained classifier counts as an adaptation, and whether McKenzie-style work memos count as commercial use, are open. GitHub PR text sits under the GitHub Terms of Service with no explicit training statement. This is not legal advice.
3. **Claude PR register transfer.** No measurement shows that Claude's PR-changelog tells predict its memo-prose tells. The overlap between the PR lexicon and the Phase 3 draft lexicon answers it in a day.
4. **Disclosure coverage and human editing.** The rate at which users hide the Claude Code footer is unknown, as is how much humans edit PR bodies after generation.
5. **Grokipedia alignment yield and representativeness.** The pilot covered 6 popular pages. Paragraph-pair yield and whether Grok's style represents Claude or GPT slop are unmeasured.
6. **Detection of 7-9B author adapters.** No published rate exists for a small open model fine-tuned on one nonfiction author. Chakrabarty's 3% comes from GPT-4o fine-tuned on literary fiction authors.
7. **Local classifier ceiling.** No study compares a WildAI-style distilled detector with Pangram on nonfiction. No ModernBERT throughput benchmark exists for M-series GPUs. Binoculars needs about 28 GB in bf16 and does not fit.
8. **Temporal shortcut size.** No paper measures how much detector accuracy comes from date cues. Adversarial debiasing for AI-text detectors is unevaluated.
9. **Same-URL refresh rates.** URL overlap and rewrite rates come from 2 domains (54 pairs) and 1 Wayback domain (17 pairs). P(AI given a heavy rewrite) is unmeasured.
10. **McKenzie's post-2022 essays.** Whether any were AI-assisted is unknown. No per-author changepoint study exists for bloggers; Kusumegi's method covers scientists' abstracts.
11. **Thresholds marked proposed.** ρ ≥ 1.5, drafter-probe AUC ≤0.60, probe AUC ≥0.90 on raw drafts and the 0.10 AUROC margin over the surface baseline have no published basis. Calibrate them on dev.
12. **Access gaps.** `pangram/editlens_Llama-3.2-3B` is access-restricted; `editlens_roberta-large` access is unchecked. Pangram's terms on distillation were not checked. He et al.'s site lists and NewsGuard's list require requests.
13. **Query drift.** Frontiers' 2026 declaration wording is unidentified, Elsevier's heading changed in June 2026, and the Codex marker vanished in December 2025. Every disclosure query needs a quarterly yield check.

## Conclusion

The bottleneck moved. AI-written informational text is now abundant and cheap to label, so finding it no longer limits quill. The limits are human judgment of what reads as AI, and evidence about quill's own adapter, which every detector-derived label is structurally blind to. That reframes the harvest. Its job is to make quill's instruments trustworthy: lexicons that survive an era control, a negative set in McKenzie's topics, and corpus metrics that a selection step cannot game. The adapter's quality question still goes to the blind tests and the user's judged outputs.

The most useful construction in this pass is cheap. Claude's own PR stream carries current, version-labeled samples of the drafter quill must erase, and the plan's half-Claude, half-local drafting already produces a content-matched attribution test. Together they make "residual Claude fingerprint" a number that can fail a release. The second idea worth keeping is the two-control rule. A tell counts only if 2024-26 AI text overuses it against same-crawl human text and against pre-2022 human text. Applied to every mined pattern, it is the cheapest available defense against the date shortcut that no paper has yet measured.
